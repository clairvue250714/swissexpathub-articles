Buying Property in Switzerland as a Foreign National

The Lex Koller Law — What You Need to Know

Switzerland has one of the most restrictive property purchase laws for foreigners in Europe — the Lex Koller, in force since 1983. Its purpose: prevent foreign speculation from driving up Swiss property prices. Depending on your residence status, the rules are completely different.

Your Situation — Three Scenarios

✅ Full rights

C Permit — No Restrictions

No restrictions. Buy any property in any canton — primary residence, holiday home, investment property to rent out. Exactly like a Swiss citizen. No special authorisation needed.

⚠ Conditions apply

B Permit — Primary Residence Only

  • The property must be for personal use — cannot be rented out
  • If you leave Switzerland and the B Permit lapses, you may be forced to sell within two years
  • Cannot purchase a holiday home at the same time

🔒 Severely restricted

No Residence Permit — Holiday Homes Only

Only holiday homes in 17 tourist cantons with annual quotas — Valais, Graubünden, Ticino, among others. Only 1,500 authorisations per year nationwide. Approval is not guaranteed.

Worth Knowing

"Switzerland has no Golden Visa. Buying a property — regardless of its value — does not confer any right of residence."

Attention — 2026 Changes

In April 2026, the Swiss Federal Council opened a public consultation to tighten the Lex Koller rules.

⚠ Proposals include: Non-EU/EFTA nationals with a B Permit would need special authorisation to buy a residence · Holiday home restrictions would tighten further · Anyone who buys and later leaves Switzerland would be obliged to sell within two years. The consultation closed in July 2026. Not yet law — but expected to advance. Anyone considering a purchase should act before the new rules take effect.

Closing Costs

In Geneva, closing costs reach 4.1% of the purchase price — the highest in the country. They include cantonal transfer tax, notary fees (mandatory in all Swiss transactions), land registry registration, and administrative charges. These costs come out of pocket before you move in — they are not part of the mortgage.

Capital Gains Tax on Sale

If you sell at a profit, you pay tax on the capital gain. In Geneva this can reach 50% of the profit if you sell within the first 2 years. It decreases progressively — the longer you hold the property, the less you pay. Those who buy to live — rather than speculate — are rarely significantly affected.

Common Mistakes

❌
Assuming B Permit gives the same freedom as C Permit
It does not. The restrictions on use and the potential obligation to sell upon leaving Switzerland are real and can have serious consequences.
❌
Underestimating closing costs
4.1% in Geneva is far above the European average. On a CHF 800k property that is CHF 32,800 out of pocket before moving in.
❌
Not checking eligibility before searching
Lex Koller may restrict the type of property you can buy. Knowing this before falling in love with an apartment you cannot purchase saves time and frustration.
❌
Not consulting a notary before signing
A notary is mandatory in every Swiss property purchase. Consulting one before — not just at closing — can prevent costly errors.

Before You Ask

Does buying property in Switzerland give me residency rights?▾
No. Switzerland has no Golden Visa programme. Purchasing a property — regardless of its value — does not confer any right of residence.
Can I rent out my property if I have a B Permit?▾
In principle, no. A B Permit only authorises the purchase of a primary residence for personal use. Renting the property out may violate the conditions of the authorisation.
Do the 2026 Lex Koller proposals affect people who already own property?▾
No. The proposals are not retroactive. Those who already own property retain their current rights.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
Before making any offer. The Lex Koller restrictions and the implications of your permit type depend very much on your specific situation.
