Buying Property in Switzerland as a Foreign National

Quick Answer: It depends on your nationality and your permit. With a C Permit — or as an EU/EFTA citizen living in Switzerland — you buy like a Swiss citizen. From outside the EU on a B Permit, only your main home. Without Swiss residence, only a holiday home, within quotas. And buying property does not give you any right of residence.

Key Takeaways

  • In Geneva, closing costs add up to about 4% of the price — paid from your own pocket, not the mortgage
  • Sell within 2 years and Geneva taxes 50% of your profit; after 25 years, 2%
  • Since April 2026, a government proposal aims to tighten the rules for buyers from outside Europe
  • Commercial property — offices, shops — falls outside Lex Koller
#1

Lex Koller — What You Need to Know

Switzerland has one of Europe's most restrictive laws on property purchases by foreigners: the Lex Koller, in force since 1983. Its purpose is to stop foreign speculation from pushing up Swiss property prices. What you can buy depends on two things: your nationality and your residence permit.

Attention — Changes Proposed in 2026

On 15 April 2026, the Federal Council opened a public consultation to tighten Lex Koller. The consultation closed on 15 July 2026. It is not law yet — the text still goes to Parliament and may change.

#3

Closing Costs

In Geneva, closing costs add up to about 4% of the purchase price: cantonal transfer tax, the notary (mandatory in every Swiss purchase), land registry and administrative fees. They come out of your own pocket before you move in — they are not part of the mortgage.

If you are buying your main home in Geneva, you can apply for Casatax, a reduction of the transfer tax that can be worth tens of thousands of francs.

Capital Gains Tax on Sale

If you sell at a profit, you pay tax on the gain. In Geneva, the rate falls the longer you have owned the property — from 50% within the first two years to 2% after 25 years. Those who buy to live in the property, rather than to speculate, are rarely hit hard.

Common Mistakes

01

Not checking eligibility before searching

Lex Koller may limit the type of property you can buy. Knowing this before falling in love with an apartment you cannot purchase saves time and frustration.

02

Only seeing the notary at signing

A notary is mandatory in every Swiss property purchase. Consulting one beforehand — not just at closing — prevents costly errors.

Before You Ask

Do the 2026 proposals affect people who already own property?▾
No. The proposals are not retroactive: existing owners keep their current rights.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
Before making any offer. Lex Koller restrictions and the effect of your permit depend very much on your specific situation.
