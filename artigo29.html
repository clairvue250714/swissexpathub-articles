Bringing Money to Switzerland: What You Need to Know

Savings, investments, the sale of a house: how to bring that money to Switzerland, what to declare and what your bank will ask for.

Quick Answer: Bringing money into Switzerland is not taxed. What Switzerland taxes is the wealth you hold each year — and the income it earns — from the moment you become resident.

What usually holds a transfer up is not the tax office but the bank: for large amounts, it will ask for proof of where the money came from. Gather those documents before you transfer.

Key Takeaways

  • Foreign accounts and investments go on your Swiss tax return; foreign property is not taxed in Switzerland, but it counts towards your tax rate
  • Online transfer services are usually cheaper than a traditional bank
  • Cash does not have to be declared when entering Switzerland — but above CHF 10,000 you must answer if customs asks
  • US citizens: the FBAR applies from US$10,000, adding up all foreign accounts

Your Swiss tax return includes everything you own abroad, at its value on 31 December — even if the money stays where it is.

How to Transfer

The most common route is an international bank transfer (SWIFT) from your bank back home to your Swiss account. For smaller amounts or frequent transfers, services such as Wise and Revolut usually offer an exchange rate 1% to 3% better.

KYC / AML

Where Did the Money Come From?

Swiss banks are required by law to check where money comes from — the KYC (“know your customer”) and AML (anti-money-laundering) rules. There is no fixed amount in law: the bank compares each incoming payment with your profile, and a transfer far above your salary or the wealth you declared when opening the account will trigger questions.

CRS

Money Not Declared Back Home

Countries automatically exchange information on bank accounts (the CRS standard): once you live in Switzerland, banks in the UK and many other countries report your accounts there to Switzerland.

US citizens

US Citizens — Their Own Rules

Bringing money to Switzerland creates no extra US tax. But there are reporting duties — and your Swiss bank already reports your accounts to the IRS under FATCA, so not filing is not an option.

Before You Ask

I inherited money from a relative who lived in the UK. Do I pay inheritance tax in Switzerland?▾
Generally not: in Switzerland, inheritance tax is charged by the canton where the deceased lived. If they lived abroad, it's up to their country. Once received, the money becomes part of the wealth you declare in Switzerland.
