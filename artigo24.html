The Swiss Tax Return: A Complete Guide for Expats in Geneva

The deadline is 31 March. The deductions are significant. Most expats miss both. Here is everything you need to know, from start to submission.

Updated August 2026
5 min read

Key Takeaways

01C Permit holders must file every year — no exceptions. B Permit holders above CHF 120,000 gross are also mandatory

Who Has to File?

🔴

C Permit — Mandatory, no exceptions

Full annual tax return required. Pays taxes directly to the tax authority. Filing is mandatory regardless of income level or complexity.

🔴

B Permit above CHF 120,000 gross — Mandatory

Ordinary assessment is required from 1 January of the year after exceeding this threshold. Below that level, tax is withheld at source by the employer.

🟢

B Permit below CHF 120,000 — Optional but often worth it

Tax is withheld automatically. Not obliged to file — but can do so voluntarily to claim deductions (Pillar 3a, professional expenses, commuting). Can result in a significant refund.

🔵

G Permit (cross-border) with quasi-resident status — Optional

Can opt for ordinary assessment in Geneva if 90%+ of household income is taxable in Switzerland. See dedicated article on quasi-resident status.

The Deadline — Earlier Than You Think

31
March

Geneva tax return deadline

The 2025 tax year return is due 31 March 2026. This is shorter than most European countries — many expats miss the first year simply because they did not know. Extensions to 30 June (or in some cases 31 December) are available on simple request to the AFC — and are almost always granted.

⚠ Request extensions before the deadline — not after. If you use a tax advisor, they request extensions automatically. If you file independently and cannot meet 31 March, submit the extension request at ge.ch/impots before the deadline passes.

How to File — Three Options

Option 1

GeTax Online

Geneva's official platform (ge.ch/impots). Free. Mainly in French. Auto-imports some data from employers and banks. Best for simple situations — single salary, no complex investments, no foreign property.

Cost: Free

Option 2

Tax Software

Third-party tools like TaxMe Online and Taxatoo. Some have English interfaces and guide you step by step. Best for medium complexity.

Cost: CHF 0–60/year

Option 3

Tax Advisor

For complex situations — foreign assets, first C Permit return, Pillar 2 buy-ins, US citizens, self-employed. The tax savings often cover the cost many times over.

Cost: CHF 300–1,500

Step by Step — GeTax Geneva

1

Create your GeTax account

Go to ge.ch/impots and create an account using your AVS number (numéro d'assuré). This number is on your health insurance card and payslip. In the first year, access may arrive by letter — request it well in advance.

2

Enter personal details and family situation

Marital status, children, whether you own or rent, canton and commune of residence. These determine which deductions are available and the applicable tax rates.

3

Declare your income

Enter the figures from your salary certificate. GeTax auto-imports some data from participating Swiss employers and banks — but always verify the figures are correct. Add investment income, rental income, and any foreign-source income.

4

Claim your deductions — the most important step

This is the section most people ignore — and the one that costs the most when missed. Enter: Pillar 3a contribution, Pillar 2 buy-in, home-to-work commuting costs, meal expenses if you cannot return home for lunch, medical costs above the threshold, and professional training costs relevant to your current role.

5

Declare your wealth

Enter the value of all assets as of 31 December — Swiss bank accounts, investment portfolios, Pillar 3a, property, and foreign assets. Deduct outstanding debts (mortgage, loans). The result is your taxable wealth.

6

Verify, submit, and archive

GeTax automatically calculates the estimated tax. Verify the figures make sense. Submit electronically. Keep a copy of the return and all supporting documents for at least 5 years — the tax authority can request documentation up to 5 years after submission.

Common Mistakes

❌
Not claiming Pillar 3a
The most common and most expensive mistake. If you contributed to Pillar 3a and did not declare it, you overpaid tax. In Geneva this saves ~CHF 2,400/year — every year missed is gone permanently.
❌
Not declaring foreign assets
Foreign bank accounts, investments, and property must be declared. Omitting them is a tax offence — and Swiss banks already share data with the tax authority under automatic exchange agreements.
❌
Forgetting interest received
Even CHF 5 in interest on a savings account must be declared. The tax authority already receives this information from Swiss banks — discrepancies trigger reviews.
❌
Requesting extensions after the deadline
Extensions must be requested before 31 March — not after. An extension requested after the deadline is rejected. If you cannot file on time, request the extension first.
❌
Not keeping supporting documents
The tax authority can request proof up to 5 years after submission. Keep all receipts, certificates, and statements in a dedicated folder — physical or digital.

✅ B Permit below CHF 120,000 — the refund most people miss: You can request a rectification (demande en rectification) by 31 March of the following year to claim deductions without filing a full tax return. Pillar 3a contributions, commuting costs, and professional expenses can result in a meaningful refund. Most people with this permit never know this option exists.

Before You Ask

Can I file the tax return in English?▾
GeTax is primarily in French. Some sections have an English version. For simple situations, third-party software like TaxMe Online offers a more complete English interface. For complex situations, a bilingual tax advisor is the most reliable option.
What happens if I don't file?▾
The tax authority issues a default assessment — an estimate of what you owe, typically calculated conservatively (in the authority's favour). There may also be fines. If you receive a default assessment, you have 30 days to contest it and submit the correct return.
I overpaid through source tax. How do I reclaim?▾
If you have a B Permit with salary below CHF 120,000, you can request a rectification (demande en rectification) by 31 March of the following year. This allows you to claim deductions — Pillar 3a, professional expenses — without filing a full tax return. Many people entitled to refunds never claim them.
When should I hire a tax advisor?▾
When it is your first year with a C Permit, when you have investments or property abroad, when you made a Pillar 2 buy-in, when you are a US citizen, or when you simply want to be sure every available deduction is claimed. The advisor's fee is itself deductible as a wealth management expense.
