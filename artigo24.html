The Swiss Tax Return: A Step-by-Step Guide for Expats in Geneva

The deadline is 31 March. The deductions are significant. Most expats miss both. Here is everything you need, from start to submission.

Quick Answer: In Geneva, last year's tax return is due on 31 March and is filed online, free of charge, with GeTax. With a C Permit it is mandatory; with a B Permit, it depends on your salary.

The step that earns you the most money is deductions; the one that causes the most trouble is forgetting assets abroad.

Key Takeaways

  • Need more time? Extensions are requested online and cost CHF 20 to 60
  • On a B Permit above CHF 120,000, a full return is mandatory from the year you cross that line
  • Below it, Pillar 3a and other deductions only count if you opt into a full return — a choice you cannot undo
  • Your Pillar 3a and pension fund balances are not part of the wealth you declare
Step 0

Who Has to File?

Find your case
Mandatory

C Permit (or Swiss citizen)

A full return every year, even if you owe no tax. Your tax is paid directly to the tax office, not withheld from your salary.

Mandatory

B Permit, above CHF 120,000 gross

Once your gross salary passes CHF 120,000 in a year, you move to what Geneva calls TOU (taxation ordinaire ultérieure): a full return from that year on, every year, for as long as you hold a B Permit. You must notify the tax office by 31 March of the following year. The tax already withheld from your salary is credited against the final bill.

Optional

B Permit, below CHF 120,000

Tax is already withheld by your employer and you don't have to file. But withholding only allows for a few flat deductions. To claim others — Pillar 3a, pension fund buy-ins, childcare costs, actual commuting costs — you must request a TOU by 31 March of the following year.

A TOU, once requested, cannot be undone: it applies to that year and every year after. There is also a "demande de rectification" (DRIS), but since 2021 it only corrects errors in your withholding — for example, a child your employer didn't take into account. It cannot be used to claim deductions.

Optional

G Permit (cross-border) with quasi-resident status

You can request a TOU in Geneva if 90% or more of your household's worldwide income is taxable in Switzerland. See our article on quasi-resident status.

Step by Step

1

Open your return

Log in at ge.ch/impots with your access codes or through your e-démarches account. In your first year, if the codes haven't arrived by February, ask for them.

2

Personal and family details

Marital status, children, whether you own or rent, your commune. This decides which deductions you can claim and which tax scale applies.

3

Declare your income

Use the figures on your salary certificate. Add investment income, rent and any foreign income. Always check the data GeTax has already filled in.

4

Claim your deductions — the most important step

This is the part most people skip — and the one that costs the most when missed: Pillar 3a, pension fund buy-ins, commuting, medical costs above the threshold, training linked to your current job. Watch out: many Geneva limits differ from the federal ones.

5

Declare your wealth

Enter the value of everything you own on 31 December — Swiss accounts, investments, property and assets abroad — and subtract your debts. The result is your taxable wealth, on which wealth tax is charged.

6

Check, submit and keep

GeTax estimates your tax. Check the figures make sense and submit online. Keep a copy and every supporting document for 10 years: if something was left out, the tax office can claim back tax up to 10 years later.

Common Mistakes

01

Not declaring your Pillar 3a

The most common and most expensive mistake. If you paid in and didn't declare it, you overpaid tax — usually CHF 1,000 to 2,500 a year, depending on income. And a missed year doesn't come back.

02

Not declaring assets abroad

Accounts, investments and property outside Switzerland must be declared. Many countries — the UK included — automatically send Switzerland the details of accounts held by Swiss residents. Leaving them out is a tax offence.

03

Forgetting interest on Swiss accounts

Interest paid by Swiss banks arrives with 35% tax already withheld (the impôt anticipé). You only get it back if you declare the account.

04

Requesting a TOU without running the numbers

On a B Permit below CHF 120,000, a TOU can bring a refund — but it can also cost more than withholding, and there is no going back. Run a simulation before you ask for it.

Before You Ask

Can I file my return in English?▾
GeTax is mainly in French, with some parts in English. For complex situations, a bilingual advisor is the safest option.
When do I actually pay the tax?▾
On a C Permit you receive provisional instalments during the year. A year's cantonal and communal tax must be fully paid by 31 March of the following year; the final bill, based on your return, arrives later with the balance.
