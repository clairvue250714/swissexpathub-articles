B Permit vs C Permit Taxation in Switzerland: What Actually Changes?

Quick Answer: Getting a C Permit does not automatically mean paying more tax. What changes is responsibility — you move from automatic payroll deductions to filing a full annual tax return. For some that is straightforward. For others it reveals financial complexity — and deductions — they never had access to before.

Key Takeaways

✓ Many expats pay similar or even less tax after the C Permit — once they understand available deductions
✓ Pillar 3a, pension fund buy-ins, childcare, and professional expenses become fully deductible
✓ Foreign investments, property, and cryptocurrency must now be declared in full
✓ The cost of professional advice is often small compared to the deductions being missed
✓ Decisions made in the first C Permit return set the pattern for every year that follows

What Actually Changes?

🔵 B Permit

Tax deducted automatically from salary
Most deductions not accessible
Limited reporting obligations
Withholding tax (impôt à la source)

🟢 C Permit

You file a full annual tax return
All deductions now accessible
Worldwide assets must be declared
Ordinary taxation applies

Once you are filing a complete tax return, deductions can have a meaningful impact on your final bill. Examples include Pillar 3a contributions, pension fund buy-ins, childcare expenses, professional expenses, insurance-related deductions, and charitable donations. The challenge is not knowing these deductions exist — it is knowing which ones apply and how to claim them.

Common Mistakes

❌

Assuming taxes automatically increase

The C Permit does not automatically raise your tax rate. Many people pay similar or even less — once they understand the deductions available.

❌

Filing without reviewing available deductions

Many expats submit their first tax return without fully understanding what they can claim. One of the most common and most avoidable mistakes.

❌

Treating the first return as a one-off

Annual filing is exactly that — annual. The habits and decisions you make in the first year tend to repeat. Starting correctly is far easier than correcting years of missed opportunities later.
