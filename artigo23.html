Wealth Tax — When It Starts to Matter

The Questions That Matter at This Level

🏛️
When to buy property — and how to structure it
The first mortgage need never be repaid. The second must be amortised in 15 years. Structuring the split correctly, and timing the purchase relative to your Pillar 2 buy-in strategy, matters significantly.
📈
How to invest the surplus tax-efficiently
Swiss capital gains on private wealth are tax-free. But wealth tax applies to the portfolio value. The balance between maximising returns and minimising wealth tax requires ongoing strategy.
🏔️
Whether canton relocation makes sense
The difference between Geneva and Zug at CHF 300,000 is over CHF 60,000/year in income tax alone. For those with geographic flexibility, this is one of the highest-value financial decisions available.
🎯
Pillar 2 in annuity or lump sum at retirement
Taking Pillar 2 as a lump sum is taxed at a reduced rate. Taking it as a monthly pension is taxed as income. The right choice depends on your overall financial picture and post-retirement plans.

Before You Ask

Is it worth moving cantons to pay less tax?▾
At CHF 300,000+, the difference between Geneva (~38% effective) and Zug (~22%) is over CHF 60,000/year in income tax alone. But moving means changing home, children's schools, and daily life. For those professionally tied to Geneva, optimising within the Geneva system is the focus. For founders, freelancers, or those with geographic flexibility, it can be transformative.
At CHF 500,000, how significant is the wealth tax?▾
After several years at this salary, total net assets can easily reach CHF 2,000,000–5,000,000. At that level, Geneva wealth tax is CHF 10,000–38,000/year — a permanent, growing annual cost that justifies dedicated planning. The two most effective tools: keeping the mortgage active (deducts from taxable wealth) and maximising Pillar 3a and Pillar 2 (fully exempt from wealth tax).
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
Immediately, if you have not already. At this income level, the cost of not having professional advice is measured in tens of thousands of francs per year — not just in taxes owed, but in Pillar 2 buy-in opportunities missed, wealth tax not optimised, and property timing not planned. The earlier you engage, the more options remain open.
Does international school still matter at this salary?▾
It is no longer a budget constraint — it is a lifestyle and educational choice. At CHF 200,000+, one or two children in international school at CHF 30,000–40,000 each represents 6–12% of gross salary. That is significant but manageable. The question shifts from "can we afford it?" to "is this the right school for our children?"
