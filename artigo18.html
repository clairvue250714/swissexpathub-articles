Retiring in Switzerland as an Expat: How the Three Pillars Add Up

Quick Answer: Your Swiss retirement income comes from three sources: the state pension (Pillar 1), your employer's pension fund (Pillar 2) and your own private savings (Pillar 3a). The first two are designed to pay around 60% of your final salary — but only if you worked your whole career in Switzerland. If you arrive at 40, you'll typically end up closer to 30%. The rest has to come from Pillar 3a and from decisions made in time.

Key Takeaways

  • Your state pension depends on how many years you contributed in Switzerland — every missing year lowers it
  • If you arrive mid-career, you can pay extra into your pension fund and deduct it from your taxes
  • Some decisions have deadlines: years before you retire or leave Switzerland
  • Leaving Switzerland doesn't mean losing what you paid in — but each pillar follows different rules

How Much You'll Actually Get

The official goal is for Pillars 1 and 2 together to replace around 60% of your final salary. That assumes a full career in Switzerland. Arrive later, and both pillars have fewer years to grow — so the result drops sharply.

You can narrow that gap in three ways: paying into Pillar 3a every year, paying extra into Pillar 2 (a "buy-in"), and choosing carefully how you take each pillar.

The Decisions, and When to Make Them

When you arrive

Open a Pillar 3a. Ask your pension fund for your buy-in capacity — how much extra you're allowed to pay in. For your first five years, the limit is 20% of your insured salary per year.

Every year

Pay into Pillar 3a by 31 December. Consider a Pillar 2 buy-in in your higher-income years, when the tax deduction is worth more.

3 years before you retire or leave

Stop buy-ins: after each one, that money is locked for three years. Tell your fund if you want a lump sum instead of a pension — many funds require advance notice.

At retirement

Choose between a lump sum and a pension for Pillar 2. Withdraw your Pillar 3a accounts in different years to pay less tax.

If you leave Switzerland

Your AHV pension is paid in the UK and the US. Pillar 2 and Pillar 3a can be withdrawn — the rules depend on where you move.

Before You Ask

If I leave Switzerland before retiring, do I lose what I paid in?▾
No. Your AHV pension is secured after one year of contributions and is paid abroad from age 65. Pillar 2 and Pillar 3a can be withdrawn or left in Switzerland. Each pillar's rules are covered in the articles below.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
When you arrive, if you're over 40 — that's when a Pillar 2 buy-in pays off most. And three to five years before you retire or leave Switzerland, when decisions about lump sums, pensions and withdrawals start to have deadlines.
