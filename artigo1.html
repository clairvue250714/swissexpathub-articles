Swiss Tax Basics for Expats in Geneva

Quick Answer: For most expats arriving in Geneva, taxes feel simple at first — salary deductions happen automatically and there is nothing obvious to worry about. The problem is what you do not know. Part of the tax deducted at source may come back to you at the end of the year — but only if you know what to claim.

Key Takeaways

  • B Permit holders are taxed at source — but that does not mean their situation is fully optimized
  • Pillar 3a contributions, professional expenses, and family costs can reduce your tax bill
  • Investments, property abroad, and cryptocurrency create additional reporting obligations
  • The first year sets the pattern — understanding the basics early prevents costly mistakes later
  • Many expats discover missed opportunities only years after arriving

Is Your Situation Simple?

For many expats on a B Permit, the answer is yes. Typical simple situations include employment income only, no significant investments, no property abroad, and no cryptocurrency holdings. In these cases, taxes are often handled largely through payroll deductions.

Four Situations That Deserve Attention

📈 You Own Investments

Brokerage accounts, ETFs, and stocks may create additional reporting considerations most expats are unaware of.

🏠 You Own Property Abroad

Foreign property often deserves additional attention from a Swiss tax perspective.

₿ You Hold Cryptocurrency

Many expats are surprised to discover that crypto can be relevant for Swiss tax purposes.

🗓️ You Plan to Stay Long Term

As your wealth, investments, and financial goals evolve, tax planning often becomes more important.

Could you be paying more tax than necessary? Part of the tax deducted at source may come back to you. If you contribute to Pillar 3a, have professional expenses including commuting costs, or certain family expenses — you may be entitled to a refund.

Before You Ask

Could I be missing deductions?▾
Potentially, yes. This depends on your circumstances. Pillar 3a contributions, commuting expenses, childcare costs, and professional expenses can all reduce your tax bill. Many B permit holders can claim deductions but never do because they were never told they existed.
I only have a foreign bank account. Is that a problem?▾
Not necessarily. However, as a Swiss tax resident, you are required to declare worldwide assets including foreign bank accounts. If the balance has never been declared, it is worth reviewing your situation with a professional.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
Many expats seek advice when they want to understand whether deductions, tax adjustments, or planning opportunities apply to their situation. The earlier you review your situation, the more options are available to you.

Many expats do not seek professional advice because they have a tax problem. They seek advice because they want confidence that they are not missing opportunities to reduce taxes, improve planning, or avoid costly mistakes.
