Opening a Bank Account in Switzerland as a Foreign National

Quick Answer: You need a Swiss bank account from day one. Your salary, rent, health insurance, and tax payments all require a Swiss IBAN. Without one, basic life in Switzerland does not work. For most expats with a professional profile, a traditional bank is the right choice. Here is what you need to know before walking into a branch.

Key Takeaways

  • You need four documents — all required, none optional: passport, residence permit, Swiss address, and employment contract
  • The employment contract is the most important document — banks need proof you will receive a regular salary
  • UBS, BCGE, and Migros Bank are the most relevant options for professional expats in Geneva
  • Migros Bank and UBS Key4 charge zero monthly fee — BCGE charges CHF 10 per month
  • Raiffeisen shareholders get cheaper banking and free museum entries — for CHF 200 to CHF 300 per share

What You Need — Four Documents, All Required

Valid passport or national ID; Swiss residence permit (B, C, L or G); Swiss residential address — the bank sends your card here; and most importantly, your employment contract — confirms you will receive a regular salary. The employment contract is the document banks check most carefully. Without it, most banks will not open the account — or will only open a limited account.

Which Bank for a Professional Expat in Geneva?

Digital banks open fast and have no fees — but do not offer mortgages, rental guarantees, or wealth management. Companies and landlords also tend to trust established banks more. For professional expats, a traditional bank is almost always the right starting point.

BCGE — Banque Cantonale de Genève: The cantonal bank of Geneva. Most experienced with international clients. Full English service. CHF 10 per month. Best for expats who want a local Geneva bank with strong English support and a long-term relationship.

UBS: Switzerland's largest bank. UBS Key4 is their digital package with zero monthly fee. Full branch access, wealth management, international transfers. Best for expats with complex international needs or significant assets.

Migros Bank: Zero monthly fee. Full traditional bank with branches. Straightforward and transparent. Best for expats who want a cost-efficient traditional bank without the premium pricing of UBS.

Raiffeisen: A cooperative bank. Buy one share for CHF 200–300 and become a member — cheaper banking, free museum entries across Switzerland, small annual dividend. Best for expats planning a longer stay who will use the cultural benefits.

📱 TWINT — Get it on day one: TWINT is Switzerland's mobile payment system. Used everywhere — markets, restaurants, splitting bills, parking. Set it up as soon as your account is active. Most expats consider it essential within the first week.

⚠ For Americans: one extra step. Under FATCA, Swiss banks must report US account holders to the IRS. Not all banks accept American clients — UBS is the most experienced. You will sign a W-9 form. Your Swiss account will almost certainly trigger FBAR reporting obligations.

Before You Ask

Can I open an account before I arrive?▾
Generally no. Most banks require a Swiss address and permit. Open your account in the first week after arrival.
Do I need a C Permit?▾
No. A B Permit is sufficient from day one.
Which bank if I have significant assets?▾
UBS for international reach and wealth management. BCGE for a Geneva relationship with strong English service and local knowledge.
I am an American. Which bank should I choose?▾
UBS is the safest choice for US citizens — they are FATCA compliant and familiar with US reporting requirements. Many smaller Swiss banks and neobanks decline US citizens due to the complexity of FATCA compliance. Always confirm before applying.
