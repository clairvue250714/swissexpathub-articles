Opening a Bank Account in Switzerland as a Foreign National

Quick Answer: Open your account in your first week — without one, nothing works. Your salary, rent, health insurance and taxes all require a Swiss IBAN. For new arrivals with a professional profile, a traditional bank is usually the better choice, not a digital one.

Key Takeaways

  • Bring your passport, residence permit and proof of a Swiss address — and, at most banks, your employment contract
  • Migros Bank and UBS's digital package charge no monthly fee; BCGE charges CHF 3 a month for its account
  • Digital banks open the same day but offer no mortgage, rental guarantee or wealth management
  • Americans sign a W-9 form — and not every Swiss bank accepts US clients
  • Set up TWINT as soon as your account is active

#1

What to Bring

The employment contract is the document banks check most carefully — particularly banks with no monthly fee: without regular salary deposits, the account has no commercial value for them. Expats who arrive with only a passport and permit often leave without an account — or with a limited one.

#2

Traditional or Digital Bank?

Digital banks open fast and have no fees — but do not offer mortgages, rental guarantees or wealth management. Companies and landlords also tend to trust established banks more. If you will need any of these services in the next 1–3 years, start with a traditional bank.

#3

Which Bank for a Professional Expat in Geneva?

BCGE

CHF 3/month

Banque Cantonale de Genève — the cantonal bank of Geneva. Highly experienced with international clients, full English service.

Best for: a local Geneva bank and a long-term relationship.

UBS

CHF 0/month

Switzerland's largest bank. Its entry-level digital package has no monthly fee, with branch access, wealth management and international transfers.

Best for: complex international needs or significant assets.

Migros Bank

CHF 0/month

No monthly fee, from the first franc. A full traditional bank with branches. Straightforward and transparent.

Best for: a low-cost traditional bank without UBS's premium pricing.

Raiffeisen

Varies

A cooperative bank; fees vary by local bank. Buy a share and you become a member. Members with a regular salary paid into the account get better banking terms, free entry to more than 500 museums across Switzerland and a small annual return.

Best for: a longer stay, if you will use the cultural benefits.

#5

For Americans: One Extra Step

Under FATCA, Swiss banks must report US account holders to the IRS. You will sign a W-9 form. Not every bank accepts American clients — UBS is the most experienced; research before you walk into a branch. Your Swiss account will almost certainly trigger FBAR reporting. See our dedicated article on FBAR and FATCA.

#6

TWINT — Get It on Day One

TWINT is Switzerland's mobile payment system, and it is used everywhere. Set it up as soon as your account is active — most expats consider it essential within the first week.

Before You Ask

Can I open an account before I arrive?▾
Generally no. Most banks require a Swiss address and residence permit. Open your account in the first week after arrival.
Do I need a C Permit?▾
No. A B Permit is enough from day one.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
If you are American, have assets in several countries or plan to apply for a mortgage. The right banking structure from day one avoids costly changes later.
