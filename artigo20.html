Financing Property in Switzerland as an Expat

Quick Answer: To buy the home you'll live in, you need at least 20% of the price in your own money and a salary that passes the bank's test. The bank lends the rest, as two mortgages.

And a big change is coming: in 2029 the tax on the "fictitious rent" of your own home ends — and so does the interest deduction. That changes the Swiss habit of never paying off the mortgage.

Key Takeaways

  • Pillar 2 can cover at most half of the minimum down payment; Pillar 3a counts as your own money
  • The mortgage up to 65% of the price never has to be repaid; the part above it must be repaid within 15 years
  • The bank works out your housing costs with a 5% interest rate, not the real one

Why Buying Pays Off

In the vast majority of cases, owning costs much less each month than renting the same home.

And it's an investment: your down payment and everything you repay stay in the home and come back to you when you sell. Rent never comes back. The risk is that prices fall, or that you have to sell early, before you've made up the costs of buying.

The Minimum Down Payment

At least 10% of the price must come from outside Pillar 2 (your workplace pension fund): savings, gifts from family or Pillar 3a (your private retirement account). The other 10% can come from Pillar 2.

Not living in Switzerland? The Lex Koller law comes first: in Geneva, in practice, you can't buy. See Buying Property in Switzerland as a Foreign National.

The One-Third Rule: How Much House You Can Afford

Your yearly housing costs can't be more than one third of your gross salary. The bank works them out with a fictitious 5% interest rate — to make sure you could still pay if rates went up — plus repayment of the 2nd mortgage and maintenance (1% of the price).

The Imputed Rental Value — Until 2028

Today, if you live in a home you own, you pay tax on a rent you never receive: the imputed rental value (valeur locative), usually 60–70% of the market rent, added to your salary. In return, you deduct interest and maintenance. That's why the Swiss rarely pay off their mortgage: the smaller the debt, the less there is to deduct.

What Changes in 2029

From 1 January 2029, the imputed rental value ends for the home you live in (approved by popular vote in September 2025). With it go the deductions for interest and maintenance.

Until 2028

✕ Tax on the fictitious rent
✓ Mortgage interest deductible
✓ Maintenance deductible

From 2029

✓ No tax on the fictitious rent
✕ Interest no longer deductible
✕ Maintenance no longer deductible

Exception: first-time buyers can deduct interest of up to CHF 10,000 (married couples) or CHF 5,000 (single) in the first year; the limit falls by 10% a year and ends after ten years.

The mortgage becomes just a cost: if your money earns more elsewhere than the interest, keep it; if it's sitting in an account, paying it off makes sense. If you buy before 2029, run the numbers for both periods.
