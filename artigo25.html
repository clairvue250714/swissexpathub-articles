Early Retirement in Switzerland: What Expats Need to Know About FIRE

High salaries make FIRE faster here than almost anywhere in Europe. But Pillar 2, wealth tax, and canton strategy change the playbook completely.

The 25× Rule — Where to Start

The core FIRE rule: accumulate 25 times your annual expenses. With a 4% annual withdrawal rate, the capital lasts indefinitely (statistically). The good news in Geneva: salaries are very high compared to the rest of Europe. A couple earning CHF 300,000 combined, living on CHF 120,000/year, saves CHF 180,000/year — and can reach FIRE in 10 to 12 years.

The Pillar 2 Problem — The Biggest Obstacle

Pillar 2 — the mandatory employer pension fund — accumulates significant capital over a career, but is locked until 5 years before the legal retirement age (currently age 60 for early withdrawal as capital). For those wanting to retire at 45 or 50, Pillar 2 is not immediately available. You need liquid capital outside of Pillar 2 to cover the interim years.

Before age 60 — Locked (if staying in Switzerland)

Capital remains in a vested benefits account (compte de libre passage). Continues to grow with investment returns. Cannot be accessed.

At any age — Available if you permanently leave Switzerland

If you permanently emigrate, you can withdraw the full Pillar 2 balance regardless of age. Many FIRE planners in Switzerland build their exit strategy around this.

From age 60 — Available in Switzerland

Can be taken as capital (taxed at reduced rate) or as monthly pension (taxed as income). The choice has significant long-term tax implications.

Pillar 3a Strategy for FIRE

Pillar 3a can be withdrawn 5 years before legal retirement age, when you leave Switzerland, when you buy your primary residence, or when you start self-employment. The key FIRE strategy: open multiple Pillar 3a accounts — up to 5 — and contribute the annual maximum (CHF 7,258 in 2026).

✅ The staggered withdrawal strategy: At the point of early retirement, withdraw one Pillar 3a account per year over multiple years. Each account is taxed separately — keeping the taxable amount per year lower and therefore the tax rate lower. The difference between withdrawing CHF 200,000 in one year vs CHF 40,000/year over 5 years can be CHF 10,000–20,000 in total tax saved.

The Canton Strategy — Where You Live at Withdrawal Matters

The canton where you are resident at the moment of withdrawal determines the tax rate on Pillar 2 and 3a capital. This is one of the most underused strategies in Swiss FIRE planning.

The Pillar 3a tax is calculated based on the canton where the account is domiciled, not necessarily where you live. This can save CHF 20,000–40,000 on a large withdrawal.

Wealth Tax — The Permanent Annual Cost

During the FIRE accumulation phase, wealth tax in Geneva grows as the portfolio grows. With CHF 2,000,000 in liquid assets, the annual wealth tax in Geneva is approximately CHF 10,000–14,000/year — a permanent, compounding cost that must be included in all FIRE calculations.

Many who reach FIRE in Switzerland choose to leave partly for this reason — eliminating wealth tax by moving to a country without it (Portugal, Spain, Italy, etc.).

Before You Ask

Can I stay in Switzerland after reaching FIRE?▾
Yes — but you must continue paying LAMal health insurance (mandatory for all residents), declare your investment portfolio annually for wealth tax, and be careful about the distinction between active income (taxable) and passive income (investments, dividends — more favourable). Many FIRE expats in Switzerland live on investment returns with no issues, but the wealth tax accumulates.
What happens to Pillar 2 if I do FIRE and stay in Switzerland?▾
The capital is transferred to a vested benefits account (Freizügigkeitsstiftung / compte de libre passage) and remains locked until age 60. It continues to grow with investment returns inside the account. You cannot access it until 60 — unless you permanently leave Switzerland.
Are ETFs a good FIRE vehicle in Switzerland?▾
Yes — Switzerland has no capital gains tax on shares held as private wealth. ETFs and stocks grow entirely tax-free until you sell. The only annual cost is wealth tax on the portfolio value. For US citizens, avoid non-US domiciled ETFs (PFIC rules) — use US-domiciled funds through a US broker instead.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
Before making any major FIRE decision — especially about Pillar 2 early withdrawal, canton strategy, and investment structure. The tax implications are significant and vary considerably with individual circumstances. A plan made 5–10 years before the target date is far more effective than one made at the last moment.
