Pillar 3a and ETFs: Can You Invest Your 3a Yourself — and How Does It Really Work?

Quick Answer: Yes — but not in a regular brokerage account. To get the tax deduction, the money must sit in a Pillar 3a pension foundation. Apps such as finpension and VIAC play that role: you open the account on your phone and choose yourself where it is invested, including in global index funds.

Key Takeaways

  • Only Pillar 3a gives you the tax deduction — many products sold as “third pillar” are 3b: they invest in ETFs or funds but do not reduce your tax
  • A fee just 0.5% higher per year can cost around CHF 59,000 over 35 years of contributions
  • finpension (0.39%), VIAC (0.40–0.44%) and frankly (0.44%) are three of the most popular low-cost digital providers in 2026
  • These platforms use Swiss index funds — not ETFs — which are exempt from Swiss stamp duty and more tax-efficient for pension savings
  • Your money is protected even if the platform goes bankrupt — assets are held in a separate regulated foundation

#1

Why You Cannot Use a Regular Brokerage Account

This is the most common misconception among expats who already invest independently.

The Swiss tax deduction for Pillar 3a is only available when the money is held in a regulated pension foundation — a specific legal structure authorised by the Swiss government. A regular brokerage account — at Interactive Brokers, DEGIRO or anywhere else — does not qualify. Some banks and brokers, such as Swissquote, offer both: their regular brokerage account does not qualify, but their 3a product does.

If you invest CHF 7,258 in ETFs through a regular brokerage account instead of a Pillar 3a account, you lose two things: the tax deduction — you cannot deduct the investment from your taxable income — and the wealth tax exemption.

In Geneva, a single person only pays wealth tax on net assets above roughly CHF 87,000. Above that line, every franc in a brokerage account is taxed every year — money in Pillar 3a is not, until you withdraw it.

#2

3a or 3b: Check What You Actually Bought

In Switzerland, “third pillar” covers two different things — and only one of them reduces your tax.

Pillar 3a (tied)

DeductionUp to CHF 7,258 a year (2026)
AccessLocked until retirement, with exceptions (buying a home, leaving Switzerland, becoming self-employed)
Wealth taxExempt until withdrawal
Held inA pension foundation or an insurer authorised for 3a

Pillar 3b (flexible)

DeductionNo 3a deduction; in Geneva only a small deduction for life insurance premiums and savings interest (CHF 2,352 for a single person)
AccessAs the contract allows
Wealth taxTaxed every year
Held inAny account, fund or insurance policy

Many banks and insurers sell products labelled “third pillar” that are in fact 3b — especially life insurance with an investment component, often offered to newly arrived expats. They may well invest in ETFs, but they do not give you the 3a deduction — and they often lock you into the contract for many years. Before you sign, check that the contract says “3a” (in French, prévoyance liée; in German, gebundene Vorsorge).

#4

ETFs vs Swiss Index Funds: What Are You Actually Buying?

This is a technical detail most expats never consider — but it matters for long-term returns.

Most digital Pillar 3a platforms do not invest in ETFs in the traditional sense. They invest in Swiss institutional index funds. Both track the same indices — global equities, US equities, Swiss equities — but the structure is different, and the reason matters:

1

Stamp duty exemption

Swiss index funds trade on the primary market — subscriptions and redemptions happen directly with the fund, not on a stock exchange. This means they are exempt from Swiss stamp duty, which applies to ETFs traded on the secondary market. Over time, this small cost difference compounds.

2

Withholding tax efficiency

Swiss pension funds can recover withholding tax on international dividends more efficiently than private investors. This is particularly relevant for US equities, where the standard withholding tax on dividends is 15–30%. Institutional Swiss funds access preferential recovery rates through specific pension fund treaties.

3

Near-zero TER on institutional tranches

Pension platforms can access institutional fund tranches not available to private investors — often with a TER close to zero. The platform fee covers everything.

#6

Is Your Money Safe If the Platform Goes Bankrupt?

This is one of the most common concerns — and the answer is reassuring.

Your Pillar 3a assets are held in a separate regulated pension foundation — legally independent from the technology platform. If finpension, VIAC or frankly were to go bankrupt, your money would not disappear: it is held in your name at a regulated custodian bank and would be transferred to another institution. Fund holdings are kept separate from the bank's own assets, and cash in the account is privileged in a bank insolvency up to CHF 100,000, on top of ordinary deposit protection.

Common Mistakes

01

Leaving Pillar 3a in a bank savings account

The tax deduction is the same regardless of provider. The difference is what happens to the money after you contribute. A savings account earning near zero is a significant missed opportunity over a 10 to 30-year investment horizon.

02

Opening only one account

You can open up to 5 Pillar 3a accounts with most providers. Spreading your balance across several accounts allows staggered withdrawals at retirement — each in a different tax year — which significantly reduces total withdrawal tax.

Before You Ask

What happens to my Pillar 3a if I leave Switzerland?▾
You can withdraw the full balance when you leave Switzerland permanently. A reduced withdrawal tax applies — and once you live abroad, it is levied by the canton where the pension foundation is based, which is why a foundation in a low-tax canton such as Schwyz can matter. The money is 100% yours from day one; there is no penalty for leaving.
I'm American. Is anything different for me?▾
Yes. The IRS may treat the Swiss funds inside a Pillar 3a as PFICs, which can mean extra US reporting (Form 8621). Read our article on PFICs before opening an account.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
If you already have an insurance-based Pillar 3a product and are unsure whether it is worth keeping.

Opening the account is the easy part. How many accounts to hold, which allocation to choose, how to plan withdrawals and how it all fits your Swiss and international tax situation depends on your circumstances.
