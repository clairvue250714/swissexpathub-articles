Cryptocurrency and Swiss Taxes: 5 Things Expats Often Miss

Quick Answer: Many expats arrive in Switzerland already owning cryptocurrency and assume that because it was purchased abroad, it has nothing to do with Swiss taxes. That assumption is worth revisiting. Switzerland is generally considered crypto-friendly — but "friendly" does not mean "invisible." The question is not whether you have a problem. It is whether everything is properly documented and declared.

Key Takeaways

  • Crypto held on foreign exchanges must be declared to the Swiss tax authority
  • In Switzerland, private investors pay no capital gains tax — but wealth tax applies
  • What matters is the total value of your holdings on 31 December, converted to CHF
  • Download your exchange statements at year-end — you may need them as proof in an audit
  • The larger your crypto holdings relative to your salary, the more a one-time review tends to be worth it

The 5 Mistakes

1

Assuming Foreign Crypto Is Outside the Swiss System

Many expats continue using exchanges they opened before arriving — Coinbase, Binance, Kraken, or others. The reasoning is understandable: the account is foreign, the crypto was purchased before moving, and nobody has asked about it. However, once you become a Swiss tax resident, your worldwide assets are relevant for declaration purposes. The location of the exchange does not change that.

2

Only Thinking About Capital Gains

In Switzerland, private investors pay no capital gains tax. Sold Bitcoin with a CHF 50,000 profit? That gain is not taxed. What does apply is wealth tax — a small percentage on the total value of your holdings at year-end. In Geneva, the rate ranges between 0.15% and 0.38%. With CHF 100,000 in crypto on 31 December, you pay roughly CHF 150 to CHF 380 per year — not on your profit, but on the total value.

3

Not Recording Your Portfolio Value at Year-End

On 31 December, the tax authority wants to know one thing: how much cryptocurrency do you hold, converted to Swiss francs. That is all. Whether it is Bitcoin, Ethereum or Tether — what matters is the total value on that day. Note that number every year. It is everything you need to declare.

4

Assuming You Will Never Need to Prove Anything

Swiss tax authorities do not have direct access to your transactions on foreign exchanges. But that does not mean you will never need to explain what you hold. What can attract attention: a large increase in wealth that does not match your salary, declared gains close to 50% of your total income, use of leverage, or instruments such as futures and CFDs — bets on price movement without owning the asset. These signals can lead the tax authority to investigate whether you are a private investor or a professional trader. The difference is significant: professional traders pay income tax on their gains.

An audit can also be triggered by something unrelated — a property transaction, an inheritance, a lifestyle that does not match declared income. The tax authority then requests your complete asset picture. Crypto enters automatically. If it was not declared or records are incomplete, the problem surfaces.

5

Not Keeping Exchange Statements

When filing, you declare the name of each exchange and the value you held on 31 December, in CHF. The tax authority accepts what you declare — no proof is required upfront. But if an audit happens, you will need to demonstrate it. Download your exchange statement at the end of each year and keep it somewhere safe. It takes five minutes and can save a great deal of trouble later.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I declare crypto held on a foreign exchange?▾
Simply declare the name of the exchange and the value you held on 31 December, converted to Swiss francs. The tax authority accepts what you declare. Keep the exchange statement as a backup in case it is ever requested.
Does crypto affect wealth tax?▾
Yes. Cryptocurrency is declared at its total value on 31 December, in CHF. In Geneva, the rate ranges between 0.15% and 0.38%. With CHF 100,000 in crypto, you pay between CHF 150 and CHF 380 per year.
What if I have held crypto for years without declaring it?▾
The issue is not just back taxes. If your crypto holdings are large relative to your salary, the tax authority will want to know where the wealth came from. Most exchanges keep full transaction history — you can export it. The problem arises when you closed an account, lost access, or the platform shut down. Either way, you will need to account for where the funds went. Speaking with an advisor early gives you the most options.
What is the most common mistake?▾
Not keeping exchange statements. If you are ever audited, you will need to present proof. Download the statement from each exchange at the end of the year and store it somewhere safe — that is all it takes.
