Wealth Tax in Geneva: When Should Expats Pay Attention?

Quick Answer: Switzerland taxes not only your income — it also taxes your net worth. But there is a critical distinction most expats miss: the growth in your investments is generally tax-free. What you pay is a small annual tax on the total value of what you own. Understanding the difference changes how you think about investing in Switzerland.

Key Takeaways

  • Capital gains on stocks and crypto are generally tax-free in Switzerland
  • Wealth tax applies to the total value of your assets — not to the gains
  • In Geneva, the effective rate is low: typically 0.15% to 0.38% per year
  • Dividends and interest are taxed as ordinary income
  • Foreign properties and investments must be declared in full

Two Separate Taxes — One Common Confusion

Most expats arrive from countries that tax capital gains. Switzerland works differently — and for long-term investors, it is more favourable:

✅ Generally Exempt
Stock and ETF price appreciation
Crypto capital gains (private investor)
Real estate capital gains
📊 Taxed
Total net worth (wealth tax — small annual %)
Dividends and interest received
Rental income

Practical Example: CHF 100,000 Portfolio

You have CHF 100,000 in ETFs (e.g. Vanguard S&P 500). By year-end the portfolio is worth CHF 108,000 — a gain of CHF 8,000. It also paid CHF 1,500 in dividends.

CHF 8,000 gain → Tax-free. No tax on the appreciation.
CHF 108,000 total → Wealth tax: ~0.15–0.38% = approximately CHF 162 to CHF 411 per year.
CHF 1,500 dividends → Taxed as income — added to your salary in your tax return.

By Asset Type: What Happens in Practice

The rules vary depending on what you hold and where it is held.

📈
Stocks & ETFs (e.g. S&P 500, Vanguard, iShares)
The most common expat investment vehicle
✅ Price appreciation: tax-free for private investors.
📊 Total portfolio value on 31 December: subject to wealth tax (~0.15–0.38% in Geneva).
💰 US dividends (Apple, Microsoft, US-domiciled ETFs): the US withholds 30% at source. Under the US-Switzerland tax treaty and form W-8BEN, this drops to 15%. The rest is reclaimed via form DA-1 on your Swiss return. Net result: you pay only your normal Swiss marginal rate — no double taxation.
💡 Irish-domiciled ETFs (ISIN starting with "IE") — such as the popular Vanguard and iShares funds available in Europe — benefit from only 15% US withholding, as the US-Ireland treaty is more favourable. This is why many Geneva expats prefer Irish ETFs.
🏠
Property Abroad (e.g. a house in London or the US)
Must be declared — but not directly taxed in Switzerland
📊 Property value: must be declared every year using the foreign tax value or purchase price. In Geneva, the official foreign valuation is used if available. The value adds to your total wealth for wealth tax purposes.
✅ Rental income: not taxed in Switzerland — it is taxed in the country where the property is located.
⚠️ Exemption with progression: the foreign property itself is not taxed in Geneva, but its value can push up the rate applied to your Swiss assets. If the rental generates a net loss, that loss cannot reduce your Swiss taxable income.
₿
Cryptocurrency (Bitcoin, Ethereum, etc.)
Gains exempt — but the symmetry cuts both ways
✅ Price appreciation: tax-free for private investors — same rules as stocks.
📊 Total value on 31 December: subject to wealth tax. The value used is the market price on that date.
⚠️ Losses are not deductible. The logic is symmetrical: because gains are exempt, losses cannot be offset. If Bitcoin dropped from CHF 20,000 to CHF 12,000, you cannot deduct the CHF 8,000 loss. If it recovers to CHF 25,000 the following year, you also pay no tax on that gain.
⚠️ These rules apply to private investors. If the tax authority determines your activity is professional — frequent trading, leverage, high turnover — gains become taxable as self-employment income. If you trade actively, seek professional advice.

Geneva Specifically

Geneva applies higher wealth tax rates than lower-tax cantons such as Zug or Schwyz — but the effective rates remain low in absolute terms.

Four Situations Where Wealth Tax Becomes Relevant

Wealth tax often starts as a distant concern and gradually becomes significant as assets grow.

📈 Your Portfolio Is Growing

ETFs, stocks and brokerage accounts are often the first assets to increase wealth tax exposure. Many expats accumulate these quietly without revisiting their obligations.

🏠 You Own Property Abroad

A house in London or the US must be declared every year. Even if no tax is paid directly on it in Switzerland, it increases your wealth base.

₿ You Hold Cryptocurrency

Gains may be tax-free, but the total holdings are assessed for wealth tax on 31 December every year — even if the year ended with a loss.

🗓️ You Have Been Here Several Years

As careers progress and assets accumulate, wealth tax becomes more relevant. Do not assume that because it was not relevant in year one, it will never be relevant.

A practical question: Instead of asking "Do I have a wealth tax problem?" ask: "If my investments continue growing over the next five to ten years, will wealth tax become relevant to me?" For most expats who have been in Geneva for more than two or three years, the answer is probably yes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is wealth tax only for millionaires?▾
Not in Geneva. With the tax-free allowance of CHF 87,872 for a single person, modest assets may not trigger any wealth tax at all. But a combination of savings, a brokerage account and a property abroad can quickly push you above that threshold — well before you would consider yourself wealthy.
Do I need to declare my UK or US bank accounts and investments?▾
Yes. Swiss tax residents must declare worldwide assets. Foreign bank accounts, investment portfolios, real estate and cryptocurrency holdings are all included, regardless of where they are held. Switzerland has automatic exchange of information agreements with over 100 countries.
My crypto lost value this year. Can I deduct the loss?▾
No. The system is symmetrical: because capital gains are exempt from income tax for private investors, losses are also not deductible. What matters for wealth tax is the market value on 31 December of each year — whether you are up or down on the year.
I have never declared my foreign investments. What now?▾
This is more common than most people realise. Switzerland has legal mechanisms to regularise undeclared assets. Speaking with an advisor before the situation becomes a formal issue is strongly recommended — the earlier you act, the more options are available.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
Usually before making significant investment, property or long-term financial decisions — and certainly before filing a full Swiss tax return for the first time. The first return sets patterns that tend to repeat in subsequent years.

Wealth tax is often the piece of the Swiss tax puzzle that expats discover too late — after assets have already grown significantly. Speaking with an advisor early helps you understand how to structure your investments efficiently from the start.
