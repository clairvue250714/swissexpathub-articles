Wealth Tax in Geneva: When Should Expats Pay Attention?

Quick Answer: Switzerland taxes not only your income — it also taxes your net worth. The important question is not whether wealth tax exists. It is whether it applies to your situation — and when. For many expats, it starts as a distant concern and gradually becomes relevant as savings, investments, and assets grow.

Key Takeaways

  • Wealth tax applies to worldwide assets — not just what you hold in Switzerland
  • It is not only for the ultra-wealthy — it becomes relevant as assets grow
  • Geneva has higher rates than most other Swiss cantons
  • Pillar 2 pension funds are exempt — Pillar 3a balances are included
  • Undeclared foreign assets can be regularized — but acting early matters

What Counts as Taxable Wealth?

Your net worth for Swiss tax purposes includes: bank accounts and savings, investment portfolios and ETFs, real estate in Switzerland and abroad, Pillar 3a balances, cryptocurrency holdings, and the cash value of life insurance policies. Mortgages and certain debts are deductible. Pillar 2 occupational pension funds are generally exempt.

Four Situations Where Wealth Tax Becomes Relevant

📈 Your Investment Portfolio Is Growing

ETFs, stocks, and brokerage accounts are often the first assets that increase wealth tax exposure. Many expats accumulate these quietly without revisiting their obligations.

🏠 You Own Property

Property ownership — whether in Switzerland or abroad — can significantly increase your net wealth position and wealth tax exposure.

₿ You Hold Cryptocurrency

Many crypto investors focus on income tax and overlook wealth tax entirely. Valuation rules can be complex, particularly for less liquid tokens.

🗓️ You Have Been Here Several Years

As careers progress and investments grow, wealth tax becomes more relevant. Do not assume that because it was not relevant in year one, it will never be relevant.

Geneva Specifically

Geneva applies higher wealth tax rates than lower-tax cantons such as Zug or Schwyz. For those already in Geneva, understanding local rates and available deductions is more useful than comparing cantons — and that is where professional advice tends to add the most value.

A practical question: Instead of asking "Do I have a wealth tax problem?" ask: "If my investments continue growing over the next five to ten years, will wealth tax become relevant to me?" For most expats who have been in Geneva for more than two or three years, the answer is probably yes.

Before You Ask

Is wealth tax only for millionaires?▾
No. It can become relevant well before most people consider themselves wealthy. A combination of savings, a brokerage account, and a property abroad can be enough to make it worth reviewing.
Do I need to declare foreign bank accounts?▾
Yes. Swiss tax residents must declare worldwide assets. Foreign accounts, investments, and property are all included regardless of where they are held.
I have never declared my foreign investments. What now?▾
This is more common than most people realize. Switzerland has legal mechanisms to regularize undeclared assets. Speaking with an advisor before the situation becomes a problem is strongly recommended.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
Usually before making significant investment, property, or long-term financial decisions — and certainly before filing a full tax return for the first time.

Many expats only discover the importance of wealth tax after their assets have grown significantly. Speaking with an advisor earlier helps you understand how wealth tax may affect your investments and long-term strategy in Switzerland.
