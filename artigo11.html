Pillar 3a in Switzerland: What It Is, How It Works and When You Can Take the Money Out

Quick Answer: Pillar 3a is a retirement savings account that the Swiss state itself encourages: everything you put in comes off your taxable income, so you pay less tax every year. In return, the money is locked until one of several situations set out in law — one of which is leaving Switzerland. For most expats it pays off, even if they only stay a few years.

What Is Pillar 3a?

The Swiss pension system rests on three pillars:

The first two pillars rarely cover enough to keep the same standard of living in retirement. Pillar 3a exists to close that gap — and it's the only one where you decide how much to put in, where to invest it and, within the rules, when to take it out.

Who Can Contribute, and How Much?

Anyone working in Switzerland with a salary subject to AHV deductions — with a B, C, L or G permit, whatever their nationality and with no minimum time in the country. In a couple, each partner can pay in their own maximum, as long as both are working.

The Money Is Locked — and When It Can Come Out

This is the most important part: once deposited, you can't withdraw the money whenever you like. It only comes out in one of these situations:

🔒
✓Retirement: from 5 years before the official retirement age — currently from age 60.
✓Leaving Switzerland for good: you withdraw the full balance, with no penalty and no minimum holding period.
✓Buying the home you'll live in: not for investment property, and only once every five years.
✓Becoming self-employed: as long as you're genuinely independent, not employed by a company.
✓Disability or death: the money can be paid to you or your beneficiaries, under specific rules.

When you withdraw, you pay withdrawal tax: a tax separate from your salary, calculated only on the amount you take out. If you already live outside Switzerland, the rate depends on the canton where your Pillar 3a provider is based — not where you used to live. Schwyz has the lowest rate; Geneva and Vaud are among the highest.

Several Accounts: A Strategy Worth Knowing

You can hold several Pillar 3a accounts — and there's a good reason to. When you withdraw, you must take each account's full balance at once, and the withdrawal tax rises with the amount. With several smaller accounts, you can withdraw them in different years and pay less tax overall. A rule of thumb: when one account approaches CHF 50,000, open another.

Before You Ask

Is there a minimum amount to deposit?▾
There's no legal minimum. You can pay in any amount up to the annual maximum, all at once or bit by bit through the year.
Can I move my Pillar 3a to another provider?▾
Yes. You can transfer the balance to another Pillar 3a provider without paying tax, as long as it goes directly from one 3a account to another.
I'm American. Can I have a Pillar 3a?▾
You can, but the US tax authority doesn't treat it as a tax-advantaged retirement account — and if you invest in funds, PFIC rules may apply. We explain this in our article on US citizens in Switzerland. Speak to an advisor before opening one.

Pillar 3a is one of the simplest and most valuable tools in Switzerland. But how much to pay in, where, how many accounts to open and when to withdraw depend on your salary, how long you plan to stay and, sometimes, your obligations in another country.
