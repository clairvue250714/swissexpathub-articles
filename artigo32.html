Pillar 1: The Swiss State Pension (AHV) — How Much Will You Actually Get?

Quick Answer: AHV is the Swiss state pension — Pillar 1. How much you get depends on two things: how many years you contributed in Switzerland and how much you earned on average. A full pension ranges from CHF 1,260 to CHF 2,520 a month, but it requires 44 years of contributions. If you arrive mid-career, you get a share of it — and the missing years can't be bought later.

Key Takeaways

  • Each missing year takes 1/44 off your pension — for good
  • From December 2026 there's a 13th pension: one extra month a year
  • UK and US citizens get their pension paid at home; contributions aren't refunded
  • The shortfall from missing years can only be made up in Pillar 2 and Pillar 3a

What Is AHV?

AHV (AVS in French) is the compulsory old-age insurance for everyone who lives or works in Switzerland. Every month, 5.3% of your gross salary goes to AHV and your employer pays another 5.3% — with no cap, even on high salaries. This money isn't saved in an account in your name: it pays today's pensions. In return, you earn the right to a monthly pension for life from age 65.

Example — Emma, from London

Emma moves to Geneva at 40, earns CHF 120,000 a year and retires at 65.

  • Her salary is above CHF 90,720 → with 44 years, her pension would be the maximum: CHF 2,520 a month
  • But she contributes 25 of 44 years: 25 ÷ 44 = 57% → 57% × CHF 2,520 ≈ CHF 1,430 a month, plus CHF 1,430 in December

Watch out: with missing years, the CHF 1,260 minimum shrinks too. Ten years on a low salary gives about 10 ÷ 44 × 1,260 ≈ CHF 290 a month. If you live in Switzerland and don't have enough to live on, you can apply for supplementary benefits (Ergänzungsleistungen / prestations complémentaires) — separate help that isn't paid to people living abroad.

Can I Pay for the Years I'm Missing?

✕

No. AHV only accepts late contributions up to five years back, and only for years when you already lived or worked in Switzerland. Years you spent in another country stay missing for good.

The shortfall is real. On CHF 5,000 a month with 20 years:

Married Couples

During the years you're married, the couple's incomes are added together and split half-and-half to calculate each person's pension. The two pensions together are capped at 150% of the maximum pension: CHF 3,780 a month.

If You Leave Switzerland

Switzerland has social security agreements with the UK and the US. For citizens of those countries, the AHV pension is paid abroad from 65, after just one year of contributions. In return, you can't ask for your contributions back when you leave.

UK · US · EU/EFTA and other agreement countries

Pension paid abroad

From 65, for life. No refund of contributions.

Countries without an agreement

Refund instead

Your contributions are paid back without interest after you leave Switzerland for good.

Example — Mike goes back to the US

Mike works 10 years in Geneva, earning above CHF 90,720, and moves back to Boston at 50.

  • His salary is above CHF 90,720 → with 44 years, his pension would be the maximum: CHF 2,520 a month
  • But he contributed 10 of 44 years: 10 ÷ 44 = 22.7% → 22.7% × CHF 2,520 ≈ CHF 570 a month, paid in the US from 65
  • His years working in the US count towards his US Social Security pension, separately

Before or After 65

Your pension can start at any age between 63 and 70. If you take it early, you generally lose 6.8% for each year — for the rest of your life. If you delay it, you get a higher amount.

Before You Ask

How do I find out my exact pension?▾
Ask your AHV compensation office for a statement of your individual account — it shows the years and salaries on record — and for an advance pension calculation. Both are free and can be requested at ahv-iv.ch.
Is the AHV pension taxed?▾
Yes. The pension, including the 13th payment, is taxable income, just like a salary.
