The Deductible — The Single Most Impactful Decision

The deductible (franchise) is the amount you pay out of pocket each year before the insurer starts paying. The higher the deductible, the lower the monthly premium. In 2026, the options are CHF 300 (minimum), CHF 500, CHF 1,000, CHF 1,500, CHF 2,000, and CHF 2,500 (maximum).

After the deductible is exhausted, you still pay 10% of all costs up to a maximum of CHF 700/year per adult. So your maximum annual out-of-pocket exposure is: deductible + CHF 700.

Accident Coverage — The 8-Hour Rule

This is one of the most misunderstood aspects of Swiss health insurance. If you work 8 or more hours per week for the same employer, your employer's accident insurance (LAA) covers both professional and non-professional accidents — including evenings, weekends, and holidays. In this case, you must remove the accident coverage from your LAMal to avoid paying twice.

⚠ The 8-hour threshold is assessed per employer — not in total. Working 6h for two employers gives you 12h total — but you are not covered for private accidents by either. Always check the payslip line "Assurance-accidents non professionnels (AANP)" — if it is there, your employer covers accidents and you should remove this coverage from LAMal.

The Subsidy Most Expats Never Claim

✅ Geneva's cantonal health insurance subsidy (IPV): If your income is below approximately CHF 80,000–120,000 gross, you may be entitled to a partial or full subsidy of your LAMal premium. Geneva's subsidy programme is the most generous in Switzerland. The application is made online at ge.ch — but it is not automatic. Many eligible expats never receive it simply because they did not know it existed.

Switching Insurers

You can switch LAMal insurer once per year, with 3 months notice before year-end (deadline: 30 November to switch from 1 January). No penalties, no health checks — the basic coverage is identical everywhere. Only the premium changes. Compare annually at priminfo.admin.ch — in Geneva, the difference between the most expensive and cheapest insurer for the same plan can be CHF 100–150/month.

Before You Ask

Can I keep my home country's insurance instead of LAMal?▾
No. LAMal is mandatory for all Swiss residents regardless of any other insurance you hold. You can have both — but LAMal is required. The only exceptions are certain cross-border workers and diplomats with specific arrangements.
Should I choose the highest deductible to save money?▾
If you are young and healthy and rarely visit the doctor — yes, the highest deductible (CHF 2,500) plus the lowest premium model is almost always cheaper on an annual total. If you use healthcare regularly, the lower deductible may cost less overall. Use the calculator above to see your maximum exposure at each level.
My employer works less than 8 hours with me. Am I covered for accidents?▾
Only for professional accidents (at work and commuting). Private accidents — evenings, weekends, sport — are covered by your LAMal. Make sure accident coverage is active in your LAMal policy. If you are unsure, check with your insurer.
Can I deduct LAMal premiums from Swiss taxes?▾
Yes — partially. In Geneva, there is a flat-rate deduction for health insurance premiums in the annual tax return. It is not the full amount paid but a fixed cantonal amount. Check the current figure in the Geneva tax declaration guide.
