Health Insurance in Switzerland: The Complete Guide for Expats

LAMal is the federal health insurance law — and the name everyone uses for compulsory basic health insurance. What you pay your insurer every month is your premium: the monthly fee for being insured, whether you see a doctor or not.

Quick Answer: Basic health insurance is compulsory, you pay it yourself, and in Geneva it costs an average of CHF 586 a month per adult. What cuts the bill most: choosing your deductible and model well, and comparing insurers every year.

Key Takeaways

  • Sign up within 3 months of arriving — your employer pays nothing towards your health insurance
  • If your medical bills are below about CHF 2,000 a year, the CHF 2,500 deductible works out cheaper overall
  • If you work 8+ hours a week for the same employer, remove accident cover: about 7% off your premium (≈ CHF 41 a month in Geneva)
  • In Geneva, the most expensive insurer charges about CHF 350 a month more than the cheapest — for exactly the same cover

#2

The Deductible — The Decision That Matters Most

The deductible (franchise) is what you pay out of your own pocket each year before your insurer starts paying. The higher the deductible, the lower your premium. For adults, the options are CHF 300 (minimum), 500, 1,000, 1,500, 2,000 and 2,500.

After the deductible, you still pay 10% of your bills (the co-payment), up to CHF 700 a year per adult. So the most you can pay out of pocket in a year is your deductible + CHF 700.

#3

Models That Lower Your Premium

On top of the deductible, you can accept a rule about which doctor you see first. Your cover stays exactly the same.

Family doctor
≈ 10–25% off

Always see the same GP first.

HMO
≈ 20% or more off

Always start at a medical centre in your insurer's network.

Telmed
≈ 7–20% off

Call a medical helpline before seeing a doctor (some answer in English).

Standard
No discount

Free choice of any doctor.

Gynaecologists, eye doctors and emergencies usually don't need to go through your first point of contact. By law, your premium can never fall below 50% of the standard premium, even with every discount combined.

#4

What Basic Insurance Does NOT Cover

  • Dental care — except in cases linked to serious illness or accidents
  • Glasses and contact lenses for adults — children up to 18 get a yearly contribution
  • Alternative medicine from therapists who aren't doctors (osteopathy, for example)
  • Treatment abroad above twice the Swiss cost — emergencies are covered up to that limit
  • Cosmetic surgery with no medical reason
  • Medicines not on the official reimbursement list
  • Covered: acupuncture, homeopathy, herbal medicine, anthroposophic and Chinese medicine — when given by a doctor trained in them

For the rest, there is supplementary insurance — optional, with its own rules.

#5

Accident Cover — The 8-Hour Rule

If you work 8 hours or more a week for the same employer, you're already covered by your employer's accident insurance (LAA) — both at work and outside it: evenings, weekends and holidays. In that case you can remove accident cover from your health insurance.

How to check: look on your payslip for the line “AANP” (assurance-accidents non professionnels — accident insurance outside work). If it's there, your employer covers you.

When you leave a job: your employer's cover ends 31 days after your contract ends. If you have no new job by then, add accident cover back to your health insurance.

Before You Ask

Can I keep my home country's insurance instead of LAMal?▾
No. LAMal is compulsory for every resident, even if you have other insurance. There are few exceptions — for example, diplomats and some international-organisation staff who have their own insurance.
Can I deduct health insurance from my taxes?▾
Partly. For Geneva tax, you deduct the premiums you actually paid, up to a cap per person. For federal tax, there is a fixed amount that combines insurance and savings interest. If you're taxed at source, a standard amount is already built into your rate.
