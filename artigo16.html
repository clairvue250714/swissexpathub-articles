Working for an International Organisation in Geneva: What It Means for Your Taxes

Quick Answer: If you work for the UN or one of Geneva's major international organisations, your salary is not subject to Swiss tax. What many don't realise is that everything else depends on two things: your nationality and the exact organisation you work for. For a foreign national at the UN, even investments and savings are exempt; for a Swiss national at the same organisation, they are taxed like any resident's. And some organisations in Geneva give no exemption at all.

What Decides Everything: Nationality and Organisation

The exemption doesn't come from ordinary Swiss tax law, but from agreements signed between Switzerland and each organisation — known as headquarters agreements. Each agreement sets out what is exempt, and most treat foreign and Swiss staff differently.

Foreign National at the UN and Similar Organisations

In plain terms: your salary is exempt, and so are your bank accounts, shares, funds and crypto — both their value and what they earn. They pay neither income tax nor wealth tax in Geneva.

There are, however, things the exemption doesn't cover:

Note: the exemption ends on the day you acquire Swiss nationality.

Swiss National at the UN and Similar Organisations

Your salary and the organisation's lump-sum benefits remain exempt. But everything else — investments, savings, crypto, property — is taxed like any resident's: income tax and wealth tax.

The 2026 ruling: in March 2026, a Geneva court ruled in favour of a Swiss WIPO employee with more than CHF 800,000 in assets: her exempt salary cannot be included in the wealth tax calculation, so she paid no tax. The tax authority has appealed — the question is not settled yet.

What About Pillar 3a?

Pillar 3a (the Swiss retirement savings account with a tax deduction) is only open to people who pay into AHV — Swiss social security. Most international organisation staff are in their organisation's own pension scheme and don't pay AHV, so they generally can't have a Pillar 3a. There are exceptions — for example, people who also have a part-time job with AHV deductions.

Common Mistakes

1

Assuming your colleague's rule applies to you

Two colleagues at the same organisation can have completely different tax situations purely because of nationality — or because one of them has since become Swiss.

2

Forgetting that your spouse doesn't share the exemption

A spouse working in Geneva with a B, C or Ci permit is taxed under the normal rules on their own salary.

3

Buying a home without budgeting for property taxes

No international organisation status exempts property in Switzerland. It's worth running the numbers before you buy.

Before You Ask

Do I have to file a tax return in Geneva?▾
If you're a foreign national at a UN-group organisation and have no property or other taxable income in Switzerland, usually not. If you're Swiss with assets, own property in Switzerland, or your organisation only exempts the salary — yes.
What changes when I become Swiss?▾
From the day you acquire Swiss nationality, your investments, savings and the income they produce become taxable. Your salary from the organisation stays exempt.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
If you hold Swiss dual nationality, are about to naturalise, work at an organisation outside the UN group, or are about to buy property. These are the situations where the general rule stops working.
