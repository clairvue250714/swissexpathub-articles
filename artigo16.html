Working for an International Organization in Geneva: What It Means for Your Taxes

Quick Answer: Geneva is home to over 40 international organizations — the UN, WHO, ILO, WTO, ICRC, CERN and hundreds more. If you work for one of them, your salary is almost certainly exempt from Swiss income tax. But that exemption does not cover everything — and many IO employees are surprised by what they still owe.

Key Takeaways

  • Salaries paid by the UN and most international organizations are exempt from Swiss income tax
  • The tax exemption covers your IO salary only — investment income, rental income, and other private income are fully taxable
  • Wealth tax still applies to your private assets — investments, property, cryptocurrency, and savings outside the IO pension fund
  • A February 2026 Geneva court ruling confirmed that IO salary cannot be factored into wealth tax calculations
  • Most IO employees cannot contribute to Pillar 3a — eligibility depends on AHV contributions

The Core Exemption: What It Covers

Officials of the UN, WHO, ILO and other specialized agencies are exempt from Swiss income tax on professional income received from the organization. This exemption is based on headquarters agreements signed between Switzerland and each international organization. In practical terms: a tax-free P-3 package of approximately USD 130,000 per year net is roughly equivalent to CHF 200,000 gross in the private sector.

What the Exemption Does NOT Cover

📈 Investment income

Dividends, interest, and capital gains from private investments are fully taxable. Your IO status does not protect them.

🏠 Rental income

If you own a property and rent it out — in Switzerland or abroad — that income is taxable.

₿ Cryptocurrency

Holdings and gains from cryptocurrency are subject to Swiss wealth tax and may generate taxable income.

💰 Private savings

Money held in private bank accounts outside the IO pension system is subject to Swiss wealth tax.

⚠ Wealth Tax: No Exemption. Even with full income tax exemption, wealth tax always applies to IO staff residing in Switzerland. In February 2026, a Geneva court ruling confirmed that IO salary cannot be factored into the canton's wealth tax calculations — this is a significant protection. But it applies to the salary, not to private assets accumulated independently.

Two IO Employees, Two Different Situations

IO Employee A — P-4, WHO

Salary USD 150,000 — fully tax-free. No private investments. No property. No cryptocurrency.

Tax situation straightforward. The exemption covers everything. No Swiss tax return required.
IO Employee B — P-5, ILO

Salary USD 190,000 — fully tax-free. Private investment portfolio CHF 400,000. Apartment in Geneva. CHF 30,000 in cryptocurrency.

Complex situation. The IO salary is exempt but investment portfolio, property, and crypto are all subject to Swiss wealth tax. A tax return is required. Which situation sounds closer to yours?

Before You Ask

Is my entire financial life tax-free because I work for an IO?▾
No. Only your salary from the organization is exempt. Private investments, savings, rental income, cryptocurrency, and property are all subject to normal Swiss taxation.
Do I need to file a Swiss tax return?▾
If your only income is from the IO and you have no private assets above the cantonal threshold — generally no. If you have private investments, property, or other income — yes.
Can I contribute to Pillar 3a?▾
Generally no — if you are covered by the IO pension system and do not pay Swiss AHV. There are exceptions. Verify with a professional.
My spouse works for a private company. Are they also exempt?▾
No. The tax exemption is personal — it applies only to the IO staff member's salary from the IO. A spouse working for a private Swiss employer pays normal Swiss income tax on their salary.
