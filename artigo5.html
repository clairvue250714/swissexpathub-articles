Quasi-Resident Status in Switzerland: Are You Missing Tax Deductions?

Quick Answer: Many professionals live in France and work in Geneva — and pay Swiss withholding tax every month without ever questioning whether they could be paying less. Quasi-Resident status allows eligible cross-border workers to file a full Swiss tax return and claim deductions not available under standard withholding tax. The challenge: most people who qualify have never heard of it.

Key Takeaways

  • Quasi-Resident status applies specifically to the cantons of Geneva and Fribourg
  • The main eligibility rule: 90% or more of your household income must be taxable in Switzerland
  • It unlocks access to Pillar 3a, pension fund buy-ins, and commuting expense deductions
  • The application deadline in Geneva is March 31 of the following tax year — missing it loses the opportunity for that year
  • Applying is not always advantageous — a simulation is strongly recommended before submitting

What Is Quasi-Resident Status?

In Switzerland, most cross-border workers are taxed at source — meaning tax is deducted directly from their salary. Under this system, the deductions available to Swiss residents are generally not accessible. Quasi-Resident status changes that. A cross-border worker can be considered quasi-resident if at least 90% of their worldwide household income is taxable in Switzerland. This allows access to ordinary subsequent taxation and the opportunity to deduct Pillar 3a contributions and other items from taxable income.

Who Is Eligible?

The core rule is straightforward: any cross-border worker whose household income is at least 90% taxable in Switzerland is potentially eligible. This threshold applies to total gross income — including professional income, rents, dividends, pensions, and other sources. Important: this status concerns only the cantons of Geneva and Fribourg. Cross-border workers attached to other cantons cannot benefit from this status.

Two Cross-Border Workers, Two Different Situations

Worker A — 98% Swiss income

Lives in France. Works in Geneva. 98% of household income comes from a Swiss salary. No significant income from French property or investments.

Has been making Pillar 3a contributions for several years but has never been able to deduct them.

Quasi-Resident status may be worth analyzing. The potential deductions — particularly Pillar 3a — could meaningfully reduce the annual tax bill.
Worker B — Rental income in France

Lives in France. Works in Geneva. Has a Swiss salary but also receives significant rental income from a property in Lyon.

Once that rental income is included, the percentage of income taxable in Switzerland may fall below 90%.

Quasi-Resident status may not be available. Which situation sounds closer to yours?

⚠ Always simulate first. Quasi-Resident status is not automatically beneficial for everyone who qualifies. Before submitting an application, it is strongly recommended to run a simulation comparing the withholding tax scenario against the ordinary taxation scenario. Once the form is submitted, the process cannot easily be reversed.

⚠ The deadline most people miss: The application must be submitted no later than March 31 of the year following the fiscal year. After this date, it will be automatically rejected except for mandatory cases.

Before You Ask

I live in France and work in Geneva. Do I automatically qualify?▾
Not automatically. The key condition is that 90% or more of your total household income must be taxable in Switzerland. If your household has significant income from French property, investments, or other sources, you may not meet the threshold.
Is applying always worth it?▾
Not always. A simulation comparing both scenarios is essential before submitting. In some cases, ordinary taxation results in a higher bill rather than a lower one.
Can I claim Pillar 3a deductions as a cross-border worker?▾
If you reside in France and hold Quasi-Resident status in the Canton of Geneva, you can apply for standard taxation in arrears, in which case you can claim deductions for Pillar 3a contributions.
Should I speak with an advisor before applying?▾
Yes — particularly because the decision is difficult to reverse and the outcome is not always positive. A professional review can confirm eligibility, run the simulation, and handle the application correctly.
