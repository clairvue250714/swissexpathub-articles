Quasi-Resident Status in Switzerland: Are You Missing Tax Deductions?

Quick Answer: Many professionals live in France and work in Geneva — and pay Swiss withholding tax every month without ever questioning whether they could be paying less. Quasi-resident status allows eligible cross-border workers to file a full Swiss tax return and claim deductions not available under standard withholding tax. The challenge: most people who qualify have never heard of it.

Key Takeaways

  • Quasi-resident status applies specifically to the cantons of Geneva and Fribourg
  • The main eligibility rule: 90% or more of your household income must be taxable in Switzerland
  • It unlocks access to Pillar 3a, pension fund buy-ins and commuting expense deductions
  • The application deadline in Geneva is 31 March of the year after the tax year — miss it and you lose the opportunity for that year
  • Applying is not always advantageous — a simulation is strongly recommended before submitting

What Is Quasi-Resident Status?

In Switzerland, most cross-border workers are taxed at source — meaning tax is deducted directly from their salary. Under this system, the deductions available to Swiss residents are generally not accessible.

Quasi-resident status changes that. A cross-border worker can be considered quasi-resident if at least 90% of their worldwide household income is taxable in Switzerland. This gives access to ordinary subsequent taxation (taxation ordinaire ultérieure, TOU) and the opportunity to deduct Pillar 3a contributions and other items from taxable income.

Who Is Eligible?

The core rule is straightforward: any cross-border worker whose household income is at least 90% taxable in Switzerland is potentially eligible. This threshold applies to total gross income — including professional income, rents, dividends, pensions and other sources.

Important: this status concerns only the cantons of Geneva and Fribourg. Cross-border workers attached to other cantons cannot benefit from it.

Before You Ask

I live in France and work in Geneva. Do I automatically qualify?▾
Not automatically. The key condition is that 90% or more of your total household income must be taxable in Switzerland. If your household has significant income from French property, investments or other sources, you may not meet the threshold.
Is applying always worth it?▾
Not always. A simulation comparing both scenarios is essential before submitting. In some cases, ordinary taxation results in a higher bill rather than a lower one.
Can I claim Pillar 3a deductions as a cross-border worker?▾
If you live in France and hold quasi-resident status in the canton of Geneva, you can apply for ordinary subsequent taxation — and in that case you can deduct your Pillar 3a contributions.
Should I speak with an advisor before applying?▾
Yes — particularly because the decision is difficult to reverse and the outcome is not always positive. A professional review can confirm eligibility, run the simulation and handle the application correctly.
