Insurance in Switzerland: What Every Expat Needs to Know

Quick Answer: In Switzerland, only health insurance is compulsory for everyone. But there's one policy almost nobody explains to expats, and it costs less than CHF 15 a month: personal liability insurance, which pays for damage you cause to other people — up to CHF 10 million. Many landlords require it before renting you a flat. Whether you need the others depends on your situation.

Key Takeaways

  • Accident insurance comes with your job — if you work at least 8 hours a week for the same employer
  • Personal liability doesn't cover damage to a car a friend or relative lends you — that needs a separate add-on
  • Contents insurance only pays off if your belongings are worth more than CHF 20,000
  • Phone insurance and annual travel insurance are rarely worth it

Basic health insurance (LAMal) is compulsory for everyone living in Switzerland and must be taken out within three months of arriving. Every insurer covers exactly the same, but prices vary a lot: in Geneva, an adult pays CHF 586 a month on average in 2026, and the cheapest costs CHF 562. Premiums rise by about 5% in 2027.

Basic insurance doesn't cover the dentist (except after accidents or serious illness), a private hospital room or treatment abroad. For those you need supplementary insurance (LCA) — which, unlike LAMal, an insurer can refuse based on your health.

How to choose your health insurance and pay less →

If you work at least 8 hours a week for the same employer, they insure you against accidents at work and outside it — evenings, weekends, skiing. You can then remove accident cover from your LAMal and pay less, usually CHF 20–40 a month. On your payslip, look for the line "AANP" (non-occupational accident insurance).

With two employers both above 8 hours, the insurer of the employer you worked for most recently before the accident pays.

Personal liability insurance (RC privée / Privathaftpflicht) pays for damage you accidentally cause to other people — injuries or broken things — anywhere in the world, up to CHF 5 or 10 million. A family policy covers everyone living in the same household. Many landlords require it before renting you a flat, and in most cantons it's compulsory if you own a dog.

Examples: breaking a friend's phone, your child breaking the neighbour's window, spilling red wine on an expensive sofa, flooding the flat below, or your dog biting someone in the park.

What personal liability doesn't cover:

✕Your own belongings — only other people's.
✕Damage at work and the company car — that's your employer's insurance.
✕A car a friend or relative lends you — damage to their car is only covered with a paid add-on, "driving third-party vehicles" (conduite de véhicules de tiers), and only for occasional use. Damage to other people and other cars is paid by the owner's compulsory motor insurance.
✕A rental car — covered by the rental company's insurance or your credit card; check before declining cover at the desk.
✕Intentional damage — never.

Contents insurance (ménage / Hausrat) covers what you own inside your flat — furniture, clothes, electronics, jewellery, bike — against theft, fire, water and natural disasters. The building has its own insurance.

CHF 20,000

Below CHF 20,000, the deductible — the part of every loss you always pay yourself — and the effort of proving what you lost cancel out most of the benefit. Watch the per-item limit: many policies only pay CHF 1,000–3,000 per item. A CHF 8,000 watch or a CHF 4,000 bike must be declared separately.

Most insurers sell liability and contents as a bundle. Online-only insurers are often cheaper than traditional ones — it's worth comparing.

Before You Ask

Does basic health insurance cover the dentist?▾
No, except after accidents or serious illness. Dental care, a private hospital room or treatment abroad require supplementary insurance (LCA), which an insurer can refuse based on your health.
If I have a skiing accident, who pays?▾
If you work at least 8 hours a week for the same employer, your employer's accident insurance. Otherwise, your LAMal — with the deductible and co-payment, as for an illness.
