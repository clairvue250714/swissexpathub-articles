Insurance in Switzerland: What Every Expat Needs to Know

One insurance costs CHF 80–150/year and covers up to CHF 10 million worldwide. Most expats have never heard of it. Here is what you actually need — and what is not worth it.

The Most Important Insurance Nobody Mentions

Personal liability insurance (RC Privée / Privathaftpflichtversicherung) is the insurance with the best cost-benefit ratio in Switzerland — and it is almost unknown among newly arrived expats.

Personal Liability Insurance (RC)

CHF 80–150 / year · worldwide coverage

You cause a flood in the flat below
You injure someone accidentally

In Geneva, with very high salaries and medical costs, a single accident injuring another person can generate a claim of CHF 100,000 to CHF 500,000 — covering medical costs, lost salary, and potentially long-term incapacity. The RC covers up to CHF 10 million. The annual premium is CHF 80 to CHF 150. It is the cheapest and most important insurance you can hold.

The Distinction Everyone Gets Wrong — Personal vs Company

This is the most common question from expats and the answer surprises many people.

✅ RC personal COVERS

  • Car borrowed from a friend or family member — YES. If you damage the friend's car, your personal RC covers the damage (if they have no casco insurance) and any third parties involved
  • Rental car — YES, in most cases, covers the excess and vehicle damage
  • Damage to third-party belongings in daily life — YES
  • Damage at someone else's home — YES
  • Valid worldwide — YES

✗ RC personal does NOT cover

  • Company car — NO. The company car is covered by the company's insurance. If you damage the company car, your personal insurance does not intervene
  • Damage caused in the course of professional activity — NO
  • Damage to your own belongings — NO, only third-party damage
  • Intentional damage — NEVER

Simple Rule

"Friend's car = your RC covers. Company car = company insurance covers. Rental car = your RC covers. When in doubt — it's probably covered."

Accident Insurance — The 8-Hour Rule

If you work 8 or more hours per week with the same employer, the employer's accident insurance covers both professional accidents and non-professional accidents (evenings, weekends, ski, cycling). In this case, you should remove the accident coverage from your LAMal — saving CHF 200–400/year.

⚠ The rule applies per employer — not in total. If you work 6h with company A and 6h with company B, you total 12h/week — but you are not covered for private accidents by either employer. Each employer is assessed separately against the 8-hour threshold.

🔵 Two employers, both above 8h/week: If you have a private accident, the insurer of the employer where you worked most recently before the accident is responsible for paying. Both employers must cover you — but only one pays per claim event.

Contents Insurance — When It's Worth It

Covers your personal belongings inside your home — furniture, clothes, electronics, jewellery — against theft, fire, water damage, and natural disasters. The building insurance covers damage to the building itself; contents insurance covers your belongings inside the apartment.

When it makes sense: if the total value of your belongings exceeds CHF 20,000. Below that value, the deductible and administrative cost of a claim rarely makes the insurance worthwhile.

✅ Tip — RC + Contents as a bundle: Most insurers sell these two together for CHF 150–400/year. Digital providers like Smile (AXA subsidiary) and Simpego cost CHF 190–215/year — significantly cheaper than traditional insurers at CHF 340–345/year. For those who don't need in-person support, digital is the most efficient choice.

⚠ Sub-limit on individual items: Most policies cap individual items at CHF 1,000–3,000. If you have a watch worth CHF 8,000 or a bicycle worth CHF 4,000, these must be declared separately — otherwise the policy only pays up to the standard sub-limit.

What Is Usually Not Worth It

📱
Phone insurance
Deductibles are high, exclusions are many, and the claims process is complicated. The RC personal already covers accidental damage to a third party's phone — yours is a different matter, rarely worth insuring separately.
✈️
Annual travel insurance
If you already have personal RC (which covers worldwide) and a good credit card with travel insurance included, a separate annual travel policy is often redundant. Check what your card already covers before buying.
🏠
Contents insurance below CHF 20,000
The deductible and effort of documenting a claim rarely justify the insurance for low-value belongings. Basic IKEA furniture + a laptop + some clothes may well be below CHF 15,000 — in which case self-insuring is more efficient.

Before You Ask

Does my RC cover me if I damage my company car?▾
No. The company car is covered by the company's insurance. Your personal RC does not intervene in situations related to your employment or company property. If you damage the company car, it is the company's insurer that handles the claim.
What if I damage a friend's car?▾
Yes, your RC covers damage to the friend's car (if they have no casco insurance) and to any third parties involved in the accident. This is precisely why personal RC is so important — it protects you even when you are using someone else's belongings.
I work part-time for two companies. Am I covered for private accidents?▾
Only if you work 8 or more hours per week for each employer individually. If you work 6h with company A and 6h with company B, you are not covered for private accidents by either — even though your total is 12h/week. The 8-hour threshold is assessed per employer, not in total. In this case, keep accident coverage in your LAMal.
Do I need contents insurance if I live in a furnished apartment?▾
The landlord's belongings are the landlord's responsibility. Your personal belongings — clothes, electronics, jewellery — are your responsibility. If the value of your personal belongings exceeds CHF 20,000, contents insurance makes sense. If it doesn't, self-insuring is usually more efficient.
