Leaving Switzerland: Taxes, Pensions and What to Do Before You Go

Leaving Switzerland is one of the best moments to recover your pension money — but the order of your decisions can save or cost tens of thousands of francs.

Quick Answer: In the year you leave, you file one last tax return, covering only the months you lived in Switzerland — and decide what to do with your pension money.

The order matters. First, move the money to a provider in a low-tax canton, while you are still in Switzerland. Then, once your departure is registered, request the payout. And before anything else, check what the tax treaty with your new country says.

Key Takeaways

  • Your Pillar 3a can be paid out in full when you leave Switzerland for good, whatever your age or destination
  • Your pension fund (Pillar 2) only comes out in full if you move outside the EU/EFTA — such as the UK or the US; moving to the EU, the mandatory part stays in Switzerland
  • Once you live abroad, payout tax is charged by the canton of the provider — and in Schwyz it is roughly half of what other cantons charge
  • Swiss health insurance (LAMal) ends on the departure date registered with your commune — no 3-month notice needed
Tax

Your Last Tax Return

In the year you leave, you only declare the period you lived in Switzerland: leave in June, and the return covers January to June. The income of those months is taxed, but the rate is set as if you had earned at that pace all year. Wealth is declared at its value on your departure date, and wealth tax is only charged for the months you were resident.

Don't wait for the usual 31 March deadline: tell the Geneva tax office (AFC) your departure date and ask for instructions for your final return.

B Permit, taxed at source? Tax keeps being withheld from your salary until you leave. To claim deductions for your final year — Pillar 3a, pension fund buy-ins — you must request a full tax return (TOU) by 31 March of the following year. Earlier years can no longer be corrected.

Pillar 3a

You Can Take It All

When you leave Switzerland for good, you can withdraw the balance of all your Pillar 3a accounts — whatever your age, your nationality or where you are going. The payout carries its own tax, separate from your income and at a reduced rate.

Strategy

The Canton Strategy

While you live in Switzerland, a payout is taxed by your canton of residence. But once your departure is registered, the tax is withheld by the canton where the paying provider is based — not the canton you lived in. And rates vary a lot.

Outside the EU/EFTA (UK, US and others)

You can withdraw all of your pension fund money, both the mandatory and the extra-mandatory part. The canton strategy applies here too.

To an EU or EFTA country

If you start paying into the new country's compulsory pension system — which happens as soon as you start working there — only the extra-mandatory part can be withdrawn. The mandatory part stays in Switzerland, in a vested benefits account (see below).

Or keep it in Switzerland

If you don't withdraw, the money goes into a vested benefits account (compte de libre passage) at a Swiss bank or foundation. It keeps growing and can be withdrawn later — from five years before retirement age. This makes sense if you might come back to Switzerland, or if your new country taxes this kind of payout heavily.

In Your New Country

The double tax treaty between Switzerland and your new country decides who taxes the payout — and whether the Swiss tax withheld can be refunded.

UK

United Kingdom

Under the Switzerland–UK treaty, lump sums from Swiss pensions are taxed only in Switzerland. The canton's withholding tax is final, and there is no UK tax on that amount — which is why the canton strategy matters so much.

US

United States

Foreign pension payouts are generally taxable income in the US. Under the treaty, the Swiss tax withheld can usually be reclaimed, and US tax is paid on the amount. Speak to a US tax specialist before any withdrawal.

EU

EU countries

Every treaty is different. In many cases, the new country taxes the payout and Switzerland refunds the tax withheld, on proof. Check the specific treaty before deciding. A note on Portugal: the non-habitual resident (NHR) regime, which favoured retirees, closed to new residents in 2024.

Before You Ask

Can I withdraw my Pillar 2 if I only leave for a while?▾
No. A withdrawal requires a permanent departure, proven by your deregistration with the commune. If you leave temporarily, the money stays in a vested benefits account and, when you work in Switzerland again, it moves into your new employer's pension fund.
How long do I have to withdraw after leaving?▾
There is no short deadline. But don't leave it too long: while the money sits there you keep paying fees — and if the rules or your country of residence change, the payout conditions can change too.
