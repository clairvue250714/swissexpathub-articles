Leaving Switzerland: Taxes, Pillar 2, and What to Do Before You Go

Leaving Switzerland is one of the best moments to recover pension capital — but the canton strategy, timing, and sequence of decisions can save or cost tens of thousands of francs.

The Exit Tax Return

In the year you leave Switzerland, you file a pro-rata tax return covering only the months you were resident. If you left in June, the return covers January to June. Income is calculated proportionally to the period of residency. Wealth, however, is declared at the date of departure — not pro-rata.

⚠ Do not wait for the normal 31 March deadline. The exit year return must be filed even if you leave before the normal deadline. Inform the AFC (Geneva tax authority) of your departure date and request specific instructions for the exit return.

Pillar 3a — You Can Withdraw Everything When You Leave

When you permanently leave Switzerland, you can withdraw the total balance of all your Pillar 3a accounts — regardless of age. This is one of the most advantageous moments for withdrawal. The tax on Pillar 3a withdrawal is calculated separately from normal income, at reduced rates. In Geneva, this tax is 8%–15% depending on the amount.

💡 The canton strategy: The withdrawal tax is calculated by the canton where the Pillar 3a account is domiciled — not where you live. Transferring accounts to a provider domiciled in Schwyz or Appenzell Innerrhoden before leaving can save CHF 10,000–30,000 depending on the balance. This transfer takes time — plan at least 6 months in advance.

Pillar 2 — Two Options When You Leave

Option 1

Withdraw as capital

Request the full withdrawal of accumulated capital. The same reduced tax as Pillar 3a applies — but on a typically much larger amount. For CHF 200,000–500,000, the tax can be CHF 20,000–80,000. Canton strategy applies here too — transfer the vested benefits account to Schwyz or Appenzell before withdrawing.

Option 2

Keep in vested benefits account

If you don't withdraw immediately, the capital transfers automatically to a vested benefits account (compte de libre passage) at a Swiss bank. It continues to grow and can be withdrawn later — at retirement age or when you emigrate definitively to a third country. Makes sense if you plan to return to Switzerland or if your destination country taxes pension withdrawals heavily.

Source Tax Refunds — Money Most People Leave Behind

✅ If you paid source tax (impôt à la source) with a B Permit and never requested a rectification, you may be entitled to a refund when you leave. Check whether deductions you were entitled to — Pillar 3a, professional expenses — were applied. If not, submit a rectification request before leaving. You can recover money from previous years.

Before You Ask

Can I withdraw Pillar 2 if I leave temporarily and return?▾
Not automatically. Withdrawal is only possible when the departure is definitive. If you return and resume professional activity in Switzerland, the capital must be returned to the pension fund. For temporary absence of up to 5 years, the capital remains in the vested benefits account.
How long do I have to withdraw Pillar 3a after leaving?▾
There is no very short legal deadline, but withdrawing in the first months after departure is recommended. While the capital remains in the account it continues to grow — but the withdrawal tax continues to be calculated in Geneva (or the canton where the account is domiciled). The sooner you act, the sooner you benefit from the lower-tax canton strategy.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
At least 6 to 12 months before you plan to leave. The canton transfer for Pillar 3a and Pillar 2, the withdrawal vs maintenance decision, and the timing all have significant tax implications that benefit from advance planning. Acting early preserves options that disappear at the last moment.
