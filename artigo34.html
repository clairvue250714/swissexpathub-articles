Tax Ruling in Geneva Before You Move: What It Is, Who Needs One and How to Get It

Quick Answer: Yes. You — or your advisor — describe your future situation in writing and how you think it should be taxed, and the Geneva tax office says whether it agrees. That confirmation is called a tax ruling, and it binds the tax office to what it signed.

Key Takeaways

  • Ask before you move or act: since September 2023, Geneva generally won't rule on what has already happened
  • Geneva's tax office decides for both cantonal and federal tax
  • Only the conclusions in your request are guaranteed, and only while the facts and the law stay the same
  • The tax office can charge up to CHF 200 an hour, on top of your advisor's fee

What Is a Tax Ruling?

It's an advance answer from the tax office:

1You describe a planned transaction or a future situation, and the tax treatment you argue for
→
2The tax office analyses it
→
✓If it agrees, it signs your request “bon pour accord” (agreed) — sometimes with conditions

It isn't a formal decision, so there's no appeal against it. But it binds the tax office under the principle of good faith (art. 9 of the Swiss Constitution), as long as the facts are as described and you disclosed everything that matters, for or against you.

Can You Ask Before You Arrive?

Yes — that's exactly what it's for. You can file in your own name or through an advisor, with a power of attorney.

Just don't leave it until afterwards: since 1 September 2023, Geneva generally no longer rules on transactions already carried out, except very recent ones whose tax return hasn't been filed yet.

Example: Mike, from Boston, arrives in July and wants to sell, in October, the shares of the company he founded. In Switzerland, gains on selling private assets are tax-free — if the tax office sees the shares as private assets, not as part of a business activity. That's a typical ruling question.

Who Usually Needs One

A US company

The Taylors, from New York, hold rental flats in an LLC (a type of US company) with two members. For the IRS, the US tax authority, it's a partnership by default: the members pay the tax, not the company. Will Geneva see the LLC the same way? A ruling answers that before the move.

An expat package

Emma, from London, is posted to Geneva by her employer for three years, with housing and her children's school paid. Three years fits within the five-year limit for expat status, which comes with its own deductions; her employer can confirm by ruling how these allowances are taxed.

3 of 5 years
Lump-sum taxation

A retired couple from London who won't work in Switzerland can ask to be taxed on what they spend, rather than on their income and wealth. The agreement usually runs for five years and is requested on arrival, at the latest before the first tax assessment becomes final.

Geneva's minimum base: CHF 400,000 a year ÷ 12 ≈ CHF 33,333 a month

How to Apply

The request is made in writing and must include all of the following, or Geneva can refuse to examine it:

  1. ✓The facts and the people involved, described accurately and in full
  2. ✓A legal and tax analysis
  3. ✓The tax treatment you're asking for
  4. ✓A conclusion listing the points the tax office must confirm
  5. ✓If your advisor files it: a power of attorney and the advisor's address for correspondence

Templates are available. Lump-sum taxation and expat allowances go to the individual-tax department; all other requests go to the tax affairs department (DAF).

How Long It Takes and What It Costs

Geneva publishes no response deadline. According to the tax office, a complete request that needs no follow-up questions moves faster — so start well before your move.

The tax office can charge according to the work involved, up to CHF 200 an hour. The advisor who prepares the request charges separately.

Illustrative figures
6 h × CHF 200 = CHF 1,200 at most

What a Ruling Doesn't Do

  • It guarantees nothing beyond the conclusions you wrote: nothing is approved tacitly.
  • It doesn't protect you if the facts or the law change: the protection ends automatically.
  • It doesn't bind the IRS or HMRC (the UK tax authority) — only the Geneva tax office, for cantonal and federal tax.

Before You Ask

Is a ruling the same as a tax simulation?▾
No. A simulation estimates how much you'll pay and binds no one; a ruling confirms how a specific situation will be taxed.
What if I end up living in Vaud?▾
A Geneva ruling doesn't apply there: each canton issues its own.
What if Geneva says no?▾
You can still file your return on your own position and, if the assessment differs, contest it in writing within 30 days.
