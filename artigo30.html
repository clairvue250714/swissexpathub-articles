Divorce and Taxes in Switzerland: What Expats Need to Know

Quick Answer: Divorce changes your taxes from the year you separate: each of you is taxed separately, for the whole year. Alimony and child support for children under 18 are deductible for the payer and taxable for the recipient.

Key Takeaways

  • Pillar 2 savings built up until the divorce filing are split in half — even if one of you never worked
  • Married without a marriage contract? If you moved to Switzerland, Swiss law usually applies from your wedding day
  • Property kept by your ex-spouse: the tax on the gain only comes when they sell
  • Married to a Swiss citizen or a C permit holder? Three years together in Switzerland and integration secure your right to stay

Your Assets: Which Law Applies

A marriage contract, similar to a prenup, is an optional document you both sign in front of a notary, before or after the wedding, to change the default rule for your assets — for example, by choosing separation of property, where each of you keeps what is in your own name. It does not change the Pillar 2 split.

If you are married and have no marriage contract, the default rule applies — participation in acquired assets (participation aux acquêts):

Pillar 2 — The Mandatory Split

Pillar 2 is your employer's pension fund. What you both built up in it between the wedding and the divorce filing is split in half, no matter who worked more. A long separation before the filing doesn't change that date.

Example (illustrative figures): during the marriage, A built up CHF 300,000 and B, who didn't work, CHF 0. Each is entitled to half: (CHF 300,000 + CHF 0) ÷ 2 = CHF 150,000, which A transfers to B's pension fund — or, if B has no fund, to a vested benefits account, which holds pension savings outside a fund.

The spouse who transferred can buy back in later: these buy-ins aren't subject to the usual limits and are tax-deductible. More on Pillar 2 buy-ins.

Alimony, Child Support and Tax

Child supportThe parent who receives it gets the child deduction and the lower tax rate for single parents. Once the child turns 18, it is no longer deductible or taxable. More on children and tax in Geneva.
Lump sumMaintenance paid in one go, agreed in the divorce settlement, is neither deductible nor taxable.

Taxed at source (B permit)? Tax withheld from your salary ignores the alimony and child support you pay: to deduct them, request a subsequent ordinary assessment (TOU) by 31 March of the following year. More on B vs C permit taxation.

Dividing Assets: When Tax Applies

Shares, ETFs and cash pass to your ex-spouse tax-free: Switzerland doesn't tax capital gains on private assets.

If one of you keeps the property and you both agree, the tax on the gain is deferred until that person sells — and the years of ownership count from the original purchase. If you sell to split the money, Geneva's real estate gains tax (IBGI) applies — and it falls the longer you've owned the property:

Before You Ask

Is our Pillar 3a split too?▾
Yes, but as marital property: under the default rule, what was saved in it during the marriage is split in half, and the judge can order the other spouse's share to be transferred to a 3a account in their name.
What about AHV, the state pension?▾
It's split too: the income you both earned in full calendar years of marriage is added together and half is credited to each of you, which raises the pension of whoever earned less. Ask any AHV compensation office for this “splitting”. More on the AHV state pension.
What if we divorce abroad?▾
Your Swiss Pillar 2 is left out of the foreign judgment: only a Swiss court can split it, in separate proceedings.
