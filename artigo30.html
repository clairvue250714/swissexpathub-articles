Divorce and Taxes in Switzerland: What Expats Need to Know

Pillar 2 splitting is mandatory. Alimony is taxable. Residence permits may be affected. The tax and legal complexity of divorce in Switzerland surprises almost every expat.

Updated August 2026
5 min read

Key Takeaways

01Pillar 2 capital accumulated during the marriage is split equally between spouses — regardless of who contributed
02Alimony paid to an ex-spouse is tax-deductible for the payer and taxable income for the recipient
03Child support is neither deductible for the payer nor taxable for the children
04Transferring assets between spouses in divorce does not trigger tax — but selling property to split the proceeds may trigger capital gains tax
05If your residence permit was linked to your marriage, divorce may affect your right to remain in Switzerland
06International couples face additional complexity — which country's law applies, and which court is competent, depend on specific circumstances

General Framework — Swiss Matrimonial Law

Divorce in Switzerland is processed by cantonal courts. The default matrimonial regime is participation in acquired assets — assets acquired during the marriage are shared equally, but pre-marital assets and individual inheritances remain with each spouse.

For expats married under a different regime (for example, separation of property under their home country's law), which regime applies depends on where the marriage was celebrated, where the couple lived, and private international law.

🔵 First step: Consult a family law attorney with international experience immediately. The rules are complex and every case is different. Do not rely on general information — the specific combination of nationalities, permits, and assets makes each divorce unique.

Pillar 2 — The Mandatory Split

This is one of the most important Swiss-specific features of divorce. Pillar 2 capital accumulated during the marriage is split equally between the two spouses — regardless of who worked or contributed more.

How the Pillar 2 split works — Example

Spouse A (worked)
CHF 150,000
⇄
Spouse B (did not work)
CHF 150,000

Total Pillar 2 accumulated during marriage: CHF 300,000. Split equally. This is a legal right in Switzerland — not negotiable without court approval.

Capital accumulated before the marriage and after the legal separation date is not split — only the capital accumulated during the matrimonial period. If the receiving spouse has no Pillar 2 of their own, the amount is transferred to a vested benefits account (compte de libre passage) in their name.

Tax on Alimony Payments

💔 Alimony to ex-spouse

For the payer: fully deductible from taxable income.

For the recipient: fully taxable as ordinary income. In Geneva, the ex-spouse receiving alimony pays income tax on it as if it were a salary.

👶 Child support

For the payer: NOT deductible.

For the children: NOT taxable. Child support is treated as a capital transfer between parents, not as income.

Tax on Asset Division

🏠
Property — potential capital gains tax
Direct transfer between spouses: no tax. If the property is sold to split the proceeds and there is a capital gain, the real estate capital gains tax (Grundstückgewinnsteuer) applies in Geneva — between 20% and 50% of the gain depending on how long the property was held.
📈
Stocks and ETFs — no capital gains tax
Transfer of shares and ETFs between spouses in divorce does not trigger capital gains tax — Switzerland has no capital gains tax for private investors. The assets enter the receiving spouse's wealth declaration from the date of transfer.
💰
Cash and bank accounts — no tax on transfer
Direct transfers of cash and bank balances between spouses are not taxable. The receiving spouse declares the balance in their wealth declaration going forward.

Residence Permit After Divorce

B Permit via spouse
After 5 years of marriage, or if there are dependent children in Switzerland, you have the right to maintain the permit independently of the divorce. Below 5 years without children, the situation requires legal advice immediately.
B Permit via employment
Not affected by divorce. Your permit is linked to your employment, not to the marriage. No action required on the permit itself.
C Permit
Not affected by divorce under any circumstances. The C Permit is an individual right accumulated over years of residency — it is independent of marital status.

⚠ If your B Permit was obtained through your spouse and you have been married less than 5 years without dependent children in Switzerland, consult an immigration lawyer immediately. The right to remain in Switzerland may not be automatic and timing matters significantly.

International Situations — When Spouses Are From Different Countries

This is the most common situation among expats in Geneva — a couple where spouses have different nationalities or one will return to their home country after the divorce. The complications include:

Which court is competent — Swiss or foreign? · Which matrimonial law applies — Swiss or the home country's? · How is alimony treated when payer and recipient live in countries with different tax systems? · How is Pillar 2 split when one spouse is non-resident? · What are the immigration implications for each spouse?

There is no single answer. Each situation requires analysis by attorneys experienced in international family law and tax — ideally working together from the beginning of the process.

The Key Principle

"In Swiss divorce, the decisions made in the first weeks — about Pillar 2, about property, about the permit — have consequences that last decades. The time to get advice is before anything is agreed, not after."

Before You Ask

My spouse never worked in Switzerland. Do they have a right to my Pillar 2?▾
Yes. The mandatory Pillar 2 split applies regardless of whether the spouse worked or not. Capital accumulated during the marriage is split equally — it is a legal right in Switzerland. The non-working spouse receives their share in a vested benefits account.
Can we agree not to split the Pillar 2?▾
In certain conditions, yes — but it requires court approval. The court can approve a different arrangement if both spouses agree and the overall solution is equitable. This typically requires demonstrating that the receiving spouse is adequately provided for through other assets. Consult an attorney.
I receive alimony from my ex-spouse. Do I pay Swiss tax on it?▾
Yes. Alimony received from an ex-spouse is taxable as ordinary income in Switzerland. It is declared in the annual tax return and taxed at the normal progressive rates — exactly like a salary. Child support, by contrast, is not taxable.
When should I speak with an advisor?▾
Immediately — as soon as divorce becomes a possibility, not after the process has started. The decisions made in the early stages have long-term fiscal and patrimonial consequences. A family lawyer with international experience and a tax advisor should work together from the outset.
