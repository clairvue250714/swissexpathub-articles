Moving to Switzerland: Financial Checklist for Expats

Quick Answer: Moving to Switzerland involves three financial priorities you cannot postpone: register within 14 days, get health insurance within 90 days, and open a Swiss bank account immediately. Missing these deadlines has real financial penalties. Everything else can follow.

Key Takeaways

  • Register with local authorities within 14 days of arrival — mandatory
  • Health insurance must be arranged within 90 days — retroactive if missed
  • Bank account needed immediately for salary, rent, and bills
  • Your employer enrolls you in Pillar 2 — verify this in your first payslip
  • Open a Pillar 3a account before 31 December of your first year

Before You Ask

What happens if I miss the 90-day health insurance deadline?▾
The canton will automatically enroll you with an insurer and backdate your premiums to your arrival date. You will also pay a penalty surcharge of up to 50% of premiums owed. There are no exceptions — secure insurance before the 90 days are up.
Which bank is best for expats in Geneva?▾
For daily banking: Neon (free, English app) or BCGE (cantonal bank, CHF 10/month, physical branches). For US citizens: UBS or Credit Suisse — many smaller banks refuse US clients due to FATCA compliance costs. Avoid opening accounts at banks that don't accept your nationality before checking.
Do I pay tax in the year I arrive?▾
Yes, from the day you become a Swiss tax resident. If you arrive mid-year, your first tax return covers only the months you lived in Switzerland. Withholding tax (impôt à la source) starts immediately from your first payslip if you have a B permit.
Can I keep my foreign bank account?▾
Yes. There is no obligation to close foreign accounts. However, you must declare all foreign accounts and assets in your Swiss wealth tax return, and US citizens must file FBAR if foreign accounts exceed $10,000.
