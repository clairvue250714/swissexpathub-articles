Children and Taxes in Geneva: How Much Can You Deduct?

Quick Answer: In Geneva, each child lowers your tax in three ways: a fixed deduction per child, a childcare deduction (up to about CHF 26,000 per child a year) and, on federal tax, CHF 263 off your bill per child. For a family with two children in childcare, the saving can top CHF 1,000 a month. But there are rules: childcare is only deductible if both parents work, and the child must be under 14.

Key Takeaways

  • Crèches, day mothers, declared babysitters and after-school care count; grandparents generally don't
  • In Geneva, claiming childcare slightly lowers your per-child deduction — but it almost always pays off
  • Family allowances (CHF 311 a month per child) are paid with your salary — and they're taxed
  • If tax is withheld from your salary (B permit), you must claim childcare by 31 March

≈ CHF 265Is claiming childcare worth it if your Geneva child deduction shrinks? Almost always. The child deduction drops by CHF 3,162 (13,698 → 10,536). Claiming pays off once childcare costs more than that a year — about CHF 265 a month.

Watch out: allowances count as income and are taxed. The Taylors receive 2 × CHF 311 = CHF 622 a month, which goes on their tax return as salary.

B Permit: Tax Withheld from Your Salary

If you have a B permit and tax is withheld at source, your child deduction is already built into the rate — your employer just needs to know how many children you have. Childcare isn't. To deduct it, you must ask the tax office for a subsequent ordinary assessment (TOU) by 31 March of the following year. If you earn more than CHF 120,000 a year, you already file a full tax return and deduct childcare there.

Before You Ask

We're separated. Who claims the deduction?▾
With shared custody, each parent gets half the Geneva child deduction (CHF 6,849) and deducts the share of childcare they paid. A parent who pays child support deducts the support payments instead of the child deduction.
What about my children's health insurance?▾
That's deductible too, but separately, under insurance premiums. In Geneva, up to a limit per child; for federal tax, within a flat amount that covers insurance and savings interest together.
