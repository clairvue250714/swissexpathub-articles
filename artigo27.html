What Is AHV / AVS?

AHV (Alters- und Hinterlassenenversicherung in German, AVS in French) is Switzerland's mandatory state pension system — Pillar 1 of the three-pillar retirement system. It operates as a pay-as-you-go system: contributions from today's workers finance pensions for today's retirees. It covers three situations: old age (retirement pension), disability (together with IV/AI), and survivors (pensions for widows and orphans).

Who Pays and How Much

Employees

5.3%

Of gross salary. The employer pays an equal 5.3% — total 10.6% on gross salary. No ceiling on contributions.

Self-employed

10.0%

Of net income. Slightly lower rate than the combined employee+employer share because there is no employer to split the cost.

Non-working residents

CHF 530+

Per year minimum. Non-working spouses, early retirees, and anyone living in Switzerland without employment must also register and pay AHV.

⚠ There is no ceiling on AHV contributions. On a salary of CHF 200,000, you pay CHF 10,600/year in AHV (plus CHF 10,600 from your employer). On CHF 300,000, you pay CHF 15,900/year. Unlike Pillar 2, there is no maximum insured salary for AHV contribution purposes.

What You Receive — The AHV Pension

The AHV pension depends on two factors: the number of years of contributions and the average income during your career. A full pension requires 44 years of contributions. Most expats who arrive mid-career will receive a proportionally reduced pension.

For most high-earning expats, the AHV pension represents a small fraction of pre-retirement income. The real retirement capital is in Pillar 2 and Pillar 3a. But the AHV pension is for life — even a reduced pension of CHF 800–1,000/month from 35 years of contributions is meaningful income in retirement.

Retirement Age and Flexibility

In 2026, the AHV retirement age is 65 years for both men and women (following the AVS 21 reform that equalised the ages). Early retirement is possible from age 63 — but with a permanent reduction in the pension. Deferral is possible up to age 70 — with a permanent increase.

What Happens to Your AHV When You Leave Switzerland

🇪🇺
Leaving for an EU/EFTA country
A social security agreement exists between Switzerland and EU/EFTA. AHV contributions made in Switzerland are recognised and combined with contributions in your destination country. At retirement, you receive a proportional pension from Switzerland (for years contributed here) and a proportional pension from your destination country.
🌍
Leaving for a non-EU/EFTA country with bilateral treaty
Switzerland has bilateral social security agreements with many countries — USA, Canada, Australia, Brazil, and others. Check whether your destination country has a treaty with Switzerland. If it does, similar rules to the EU/EFTA arrangement may apply.
❓
No treaty — contributions stay in Switzerland
Your AHV contributions remain "held" in Switzerland. When you reach Swiss retirement age (65), you receive the proportional pension for the years you contributed — even if you live abroad. The pension is paid to your foreign bank account.

✅ Can you claim a refund of AHV contributions? Yes — but only if you are a citizen of a country outside the EU/EFTA with no bilateral treaty. The refund is an option — but you permanently lose the right to the future pension. For most people, the lifetime pension value exceeds the refund amount.

Before You Ask

I contribute to AHV but will never retire in Switzerland. Is it worth it?▾
It is mandatory while you work in Switzerland — there is no choice. But the capital does not disappear: you either receive a proportional pension at retirement age, or (in certain conditions) can request a refund. It is money that remains yours.
My spouse does not work. Do they need to pay AHV?▾
Yes, if they live in Switzerland. Non-working spouses must register with the cantonal compensation office and pay the minimum contribution (CHF 530/year in 2026). Failing to do so means those years are uninsured and reduce the future pension.
How much AHV will I receive if I work in Switzerland for 15 years?▾
15 years corresponds to 15/44 of the full pension. At a high income, the full pension would be CHF 2,450/month. With 15 years: approximately CHF 835/month at age 65. A fraction of the maximum — but paid for life.
