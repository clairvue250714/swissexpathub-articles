Cost of Living in Geneva on CHF 120,000–200,000 a Year

The question is no longer whether you can afford Geneva. It is where it makes sense to spend — and where you are leaving money on the table.

Quick Answer: On CHF 120,000 to 200,000 gross a year, a single person in Geneva takes home roughly CHF 7,000 to 10,700 a month. That is a comfortable life — until international school enters the picture.

At this level, what moves your budget most is not groceries or restaurants: it is tax (and the deductions that reduce it) and, if you choose a private school for your children, school fees. Geneva's public schools are free.

Key Takeaways

  • On CHF 150,000 gross, about CHF 8,400 a month is left after tax and social contributions
  • On a B Permit above CHF 120,000, a full tax return becomes mandatory — and opens up every deduction
  • A CHF 40,000 pension fund buy-in can cut your tax by about CHF 14,000 in a single year
  • A couple without children earning CHF 300,000 together can save around CHF 6,000–6,500 a month

Housing

More Options, Still Expensive

A common rule is to spend no more than 30% of take-home pay on rent. On CHF 150,000 gross that is about CHF 2,500 a month (30% of 8,400) — enough for a good 2-bedroom flat in many neighbourhoods, but not all.

Bringing your family? If your spouse and children join you on a residence permit, Geneva's population office (OCPM) checks that the flat is big enough. There is no fixed table, but in practice it looks for about one room per person, kitchen included: a couple with two children should look for at least a "4-pièces".

Taxes

The Most Important Variable at This Level

At CHF 120,000–200,000, tax stops being a background cost and becomes your main financial lever. The share of gross salary going to tax and social contributions climbs from about 30% to about 36%.

The number that matters for planning is your marginal rate — the tax on the last francs you earn. In Geneva at this level it is around 35% in income tax alone. That is the rate every deduction "saves".

B Permit above CHF 120,000: tax is no longer just withheld from your salary — you file a full tax return every year (known as TOU, taxation ordinaire ultérieure). More paperwork, but it unlocks all the deductions below.

Deductions

The Two That Actually Move the Needle

Pillar 3a — CHF 7,258 per year

A tax-advantaged retirement savings account: what you pay in comes off that year's taxable income. It is the simplest deduction available — just pay in the maximum before 31 December. At a marginal rate of about 35%: 7,258 × 35% ≈ CHF 2,540 less tax every year.

Pension fund buy-in (Pillar 2) — no fixed cap

Anyone who arrives mid-career usually has a "gap" in their employer's pension fund — often CHF 50,000–200,000, shown on the fund's annual statement. You can fill it voluntarily, and every franc is deductible.

Schools

The Decision That Changes Everything

First: public school is free. Geneva's public schools teach in French. Many expat families choose them, especially with young children, who pick up the language quickly. International school is an option — not an obligation.

If you choose international school, it is affordable at CHF 120,000–200,000 — but it dominates the budget. The main options, per child, for the 2026–27 school year:

The maths on two children: on CHF 150,000 gross, two children at a mid-range school cost

2 × CHF 30,000 = CHF 60,000 a year · 60,000 ÷ 150,000 = 40% of gross salary — before tax, rent or health insurance.

School fees paid by your employer: they are only tax-free if you are an "expatriate" under Swiss tax law — sent to Switzerland by a foreign employer for at most five years, with children at a school in their own language. If you were hired locally, employer-paid fees count as salary and are taxed. It can still be worth negotiating: many employers agree to cover fees more readily than a pay rise.

Before You Ask

Which health insurance deductible should I choose?▾
The deductible (franchise) is what you pay out of your own pocket each year before the insurer starts paying. The higher the deductible, the cheaper the monthly premium. In Geneva, an adult on the minimum CHF 300 deductible pays on average about CHF 586 a month in 2026; with the maximum CHF 2,500 deductible, the premium drops considerably.

If you are young, healthy and rarely see a doctor, the high deductible is usually the better choice. The low deductible pays off for people who use the health system more — with age or a chronic condition. As a rule of thumb, the CHF 300 deductible starts to pay off from about CHF 1,800–1,900 of medical costs a year.
When should I speak with a tax advisor?▾
At this income level, as early as possible. The sooner you start, the more years of maximum Pillar 3a you build up — and a pension fund buy-in needs to be planned with the 3-year rule in mind.

At CHF 120,000–200,000, the difference between a well-planned set-up and no plan at all is measured in tens of thousands of francs a year.
